# Long help text: HELP <command> and /? <command>

For 0.22.0. Written 2026-09-22 against the working tree (0.21.9 in
progress) plus the three additions specified for 0.22.0: BELL, CODES and
INFO with their room forms. Text only; nothing in src/ was changed.

How to read this file:

- A block starts at a line beginning `== ` and ends at the first blank
  line. Everything outside a block, including the fence lines and the
  headings, is a note for the builder and is not help text.
- The `== ` line carries the verb as it is typed. Room commands keep their
  slash.
- `usage:` is the syntax. Square brackets mark what is optional; plain
  words are required. Alternatives are written with "or", never with a
  bar, because a C64 has no bar.
- Then 1 to 5 lines of description.
- Every line inside a block, the `usage:` line included, is 39 characters
  or fewer. Checked by script against the ruler on line 1, not by eye.
- Plain ASCII only inside blocks, and none of the characters a C64 lacks
  (braces, bar, tilde, backslash, underscore, caret, backtick).
- Blocks that contain @-codes (BELL, CODES, /codes) must be printed
  literally. See the notes at the end.

## Main prompt: everybody

```text
== WHO
usage: WHO [n]
Who is on each node: rank mark, handle,
terminal, minutes on and idle time.
WHO n redraws the list every n seconds
until you press a key. W also works.

== PAGE
usage: PAGE n message
Sends one line to the caller on node n.
It arrives with a bell when they are
next at the main prompt, not while they
are in chat, files or forums.
Callers with DND on do not take pages.

== G
usage: G
Logs off, asking Log off (Y/N)? first.
BYE logs off without asking.

== BYE
usage: BYE [password]
Logs off now. OFF, LOGOFF and QUIT too.
BYE with a staff password raises your
access instead. A wrong password logs
you off; 3 wrong in 15 minutes lock
your address out for 15 minutes.

== HELP
usage: HELP [menu or command]
The menus. ? and H do the same.
Menus: CHAT, ACCOUNT, STAFF, SYSOP,
and ALL for every menu in turn.
HELP with a command, such as HELP WHO,
explains that one command.

== FORUMS
usage: FORUMS
The message boards, by topic.
Enter reads the next unread message.
A number opens a forum or a subject;
P posts, R replies, ? lists the keys
and Q goes back.

== FILES
usage: FILES [n]
The file areas. FILES n opens area n.
In an area, L lists, a number downloads
that file, U uploads, ? shows the keys
and Q goes back. Uploads wait for staff
to approve them. F also works.

== CHAT
usage: CHAT
Joins the chat room. What you type goes
to everyone there. /? lists the room's
commands and /q leaves. ESC clears the
line you are typing; it does not leave.

== MAIL
usage: MAIL [handle] [text]
Your mailbox: a list, * for new. A
number reads one, Enter the oldest new,
W writes, Q leaves. MAIL handle writes
to them, MAIL handle text sends a line.
Needs an account and is not private.

== WHOIS
usage: WHOIS [handle]
A caller's profile: name, member since,
last call, calls and profile text.
With no handle it shows your own.
Email, From and Phone show only on your
own, or to staff who manage accounts.

== TIME
usage: TIME [n +m or n -m]
The date, time on, and time left.
Staff: TIME n +m gives node n more
minutes, TIME n -m takes some away.
TIME n -1 is no limit for that call,
not one minute less. TIME -1 is you.

== LAST
usage: LAST
The last 50 calls, newest first, with
handle, node, when and minutes on.
Guests are marked with a *.

== CALLS
usage: CALLS
Calls by hour of day, as a bar chart,
from the last 50 calls. It shows when
the board is busy, and names nobody.

== INFO
usage: INFO [n] [EDIT or CLEAR]
The sysop's information pages, 0 to 9.
INFO lists them and INFO n reads one.
I also works; /i and /i3 in the room.
Sysop: INFO n EDIT writes page n in the
editor and INFO n CLEAR empties it.

== CODES
usage: CODES
The @-codes you can put in forum posts,
mail and chat lines: colours such as
@RED@, effects such as @BLINK:text@,
and @BELL@, @BOARD@, @DATE@, @TIME@.
/codes in the room shows the same.

== BELL
usage: BELL
Turns the bell on or off for this call.
It covers bells other people ring:
pages, broadcasts, the chat room and
@BELL@ in a message. /b in the chat
room is the same switch.

== DND
usage: DND
Pages to you off, or back on again.
Somebody who pages you is told you are
not taking pages. BELL is the gentler
choice: pages arrive, without the bell.

== PROFILE
usage: PROFILE
Your details: name, email, where you
are from, phone, and profile text.
Start picks where login puts you.
Email, From and Phone are seen only by
you and staff who manage accounts.

== PASSWORD
usage: PASSWORD
Current password first, then the new
one twice. The line is not encrypted,
so use a password you use nowhere else.

== PRIVACY
usage: PRIVACY
What the board keeps about you, who can
see it, and what it means that the line
is not encrypted. It is the screen
offered when you sign up.

== TERM
usage: TERM
Your terminal as the board sees it:
type, width and height, telnet on or
off, and line speed. T also works.

== BAUD
usage: BAUD n, or BAUD OFF
Slows the board's output to the speed
of an old modem: 300, 1200, 2400, 9600
or 19200. BAUD OFF is full speed again.
Only your line slows down.

== CLS
usage: CLS
Clears the screen. C also works.

== MEM
usage: MEM
The board's memory: free heap, the
lowest it has been, session size, busy
nodes, and free space on flash and on
the card if it has one. M also works.

== ABOUT
usage: ABOUT
The board's about screen: the software,
its version and its licence.

== FX
usage: FX
A demo of the screen effects the board
can do. Any key skips a step; Space or
ESC stops it. On a C64, Space or the
left arrow key.

```

## Main prompt: staff

Who may run each of these comes from the `[access]` matrix, and a command
a caller may not run should get the same answer from `HELP <command>` as
the command itself gives: unknown. See the notes at the end.

```text
== DASH
usage: DASH [n]
The whole board on one screen: time,
memory, each node and what it is doing,
bans, Wi-Fi signal and the last calls.
DASH n redraws it every n seconds until
you press a key.

== NODES
usage: NODES [n]
Every session, busy line and sysop node
included, with handle, address, minutes
left and idle time. NODES n redraws it
every n seconds until you press a key.

== KICK
usage: KICK n [message]
Disconnects the caller on node n. They
see Disconnected by sysop, with your
message if you give one. Works on
callers and on staff below your rank.

== SNOOP
usage: SNOOP n
Shows you everything node n sees, as it
happens. Q, ESC or Ctrl-C stops.
Your terminal type must match theirs,
one watcher per node, and only callers
or staff below your rank.

== BROADCAST
usage: BROADCAST message
Sends a line to every caller on the
board, with a bell and a flashing tag.
Each caller sees it when they are next
at the main prompt.

== USERS
usage: USERS
The account manager. Cursor keys move,
Enter edits, A adds, D retires and Q
quits. Plain ASCII gets a list instead;
use USER ADD, EDIT and DEL there.
Only accounts at your rank or below.

== USER
usage: USER ADD, EDIT h or DEL h
ADD opens a form for a new account.
EDIT h opens account h; leave New pass
blank to keep its password.
DEL h retires it: no more logins, and
the handle is never given out again.

== HIDE
usage: HIDE
Takes you out of WHO. To callers your
line looks free. SHOW puts you back.

== SHOW
usage: SHOW
Lists you in WHO again and turns pages
back on. HIDE and LURK take you out.

== LURK
usage: LURK
Hidden from WHO and not taking pages.
LURK again turns pages back on but
leaves you hidden; SHOW lists you.

== BANS
usage: BANS
The addresses banned for wrong staff
passwords, with the minutes left on
each. UNBAN lifts one.

== UNBAN
usage: UNBAN address
Lifts the ban on one address, written
as four numbers, such as 192.168.1.20.
BANS lists what is banned.

== DROP
usage: DROP
Gives up your staff access for the rest
of this call. The sysop moves back to a
free caller node. Time limits start
again from now.

== SYS
usage: SYS
The board's health on one screen:
Wi-Fi, memory, storage, load and
traffic, with the slowest pass of the
loop and which part of it was slow.
Any staff level can use it.

== PLUGINS
usage: PLUGINS
Every plugin built into this board, its
version, and whether it is running or
why not. Also free space on flash.

```

## Main prompt: sysop

`CONFIG` and `SHUTDOWN` are the sysop's whatever the matrix says. `SD`,
`ANNOUNCE` and `CHATCLEAR` follow their plugin's admin level, which is the
sysop by default.

```text
== CONFIG
usage: CONFIG [page]
The board's settings, page by page.
CONFIG lists the pages and CONFIG with
a page name, such as CONFIG chat, opens
it. F1 saves, and most changes apply
at once. Sysop only, never co-sysops.

== SHUTDOWN
usage: SHUTDOWN [n or CANCEL]
Warns everyone, counts down n seconds
(5 to 3600, default 60), then hangs up
on all callers, you too, and any file
transfer is lost. SHUTDOWN CANCEL stops
it. Only a restart brings it back.

== SD
usage: SD [MOUNT or UNMOUNT]
The SD card: type, free space and where
screens come from, or the pins tried if
there is no card. SD MOUNT pauses the
board while it mounts; SD UNMOUNT makes
the card safe to pull.

== ANNOUNCE
usage: ANNOUNCE [TEST or NOW]
Whether the board is listed in a
directory and what each one said last.
ANNOUNCE TEST prints exactly what would
be sent and sends nothing; ANNOUNCE NOW
sends it straight away.

== CHATCLEAR
usage: CHATCLEAR
Empties the chat room's memory, so new
arrivals see no earlier lines and /sh
has nothing to replay.

== SERIAL
usage: SERIAL [STATUS or SET]
Watches the device on the serial port.
T takes the keyboard when it is free,
if you are allowed; ESC leaves.
SERIAL STATUS shows the port settings;
SERIAL SET 9600 8N1 changes them.

== LIGHTS
usage: LIGHTS [TEST]
The lights plugin's two outputs: each
one's pin, effect and brightness, and
the colours it was last sent, in hex.
LIGHTS TEST shows red, green, blue and
white on every pixel, a second each.

```

`SERIAL` sits here because the serial plugin is off until a sysop switches
it on, and `SET` needs its write level (staff by default). Watching is open
to everybody once it is on.

## Chat room

```text
== /?
usage: /? [command]
Lists the room's commands; staff see
theirs too. /? and a command, such as
/? p, explains that one. /help and /h
also work.

== /s
usage: /s
Who is in the room, with away notes.
/w and /who also work.

== /p
usage: /p n text, or /p n*
One line to node n in the room, seen
by them alone. /p n* sticks: all you
type goes to n, and [>n] shows on your
input line until /p* ends it.
/pm and /msg also work.

== /me
usage: /me text
An action line: /me waves shows your
handle between asterisks, as in
** Daytona waves **.
Up to five every 30 seconds.

== /page
usage: /page n why
Pages node n with a bell, the same as
PAGE. They see it when they are next
at the main prompt, so for somebody in
the room /p gets there sooner.
/pg also works.

== /whois
usage: /whois handle
A caller's profile, as WHOIS shows it,
without leaving the room. Email, From
and Phone show only on your own.
/wi also works.

== /a
usage: /a [note]
Marks you away, with a note of up to 16
characters. The room is told. /a on its
own marks you back. /away also works.

== /sq
usage: /sq n
Hides what node n says until you leave
the room or type /sq n again. Arrivals,
departures and room notices still show.
/squelch also works.

== /email
usage: /email handle message
Leaves a one-line message for a caller
to read later, as MAIL does at the main
prompt. Needs an account at both ends.
/em also works.

== /e
usage: /e
Reads your oldest message, then asks
Reply, Save or Delete. Enter or ESC
leaves it unread. /mail also works.

== /sh
usage: /sh [n]
Replays the last n lines said in the
room, 20 if you leave n out. Nodes you
have hidden with /sq stay hidden.
/history and /scroll also work.

== /t
usage: /t [n +m or n -m]
The time, and your minutes left this
call. Staff: /t n +m and /t n -m give
or take node n's minutes, as TIME does,
and /t n -1 means no limit that call.
/time also works.

== /b
usage: /b, or /b handle
Turns the bell on or off: the same
switch as BELL. /bell also works.
Staff: /b handle bars that handle from
the room and puts them out if they are
in it. /ban handle does the same.

== /i
usage: /i [n]
The information pages: /i lists them,
/i3 reads page 3, the same as INFO.
Sysop: /i3- empties page 3. Writing
one is INFO 3 EDIT at the main prompt.

== /codes
usage: /codes
The @-codes you can use in chat lines,
mail and forum posts, such as @RED@ or
@BLINK:text@. The same list as CODES.

== /clear
usage: /clear
Clears your screen, not anyone else's.
/cls also works.

== /welcome
usage: /welcome
Shows the room's welcome screen again,
if this board has one.
/intro also works.

== /q
usage: /q
Leaves the room for the main prompt.
/quit also works. ESC does not leave:
it clears the line you are typing.

== /q+
usage: /q+
Leaves the room and logs off in one go,
with the same send-off as BYE. The room
sees you log off. /quit+ also works.

== /vk
usage: /vk n
Votes to put node n out of the room.
Works with no staff present and three
or more in the room. Two thirds of the
others must vote within 60 seconds.
They can come back. /votekick works.

```

## Notes for the builder (not help text)

### Entry count

75 blocks: 52 main prompt verbs (26 for everybody, 15 staff, 6 sysop with
SERIAL counted there, 5 example plugin) and 23 room commands (20 for
everybody, `/b` among them with its staff half inside it, and 3 staff).
Each `==` verb appears once. Verbs with several forms in the command table
(`TIME`, `USER`, `SD`, `ANNOUNCE`) are one block each.

### Things the lookup has to get right

- **Print the blocks literally.** BELL, CODES and /codes contain `@RED@`,
  `@BLINK:text@` and `@BELL@`. If help text goes through the @-code
  expander, `HELP BELL` rings the bell and `HELP CODES` turns itself red
  and shows none of the codes it is meant to list.
- **Answer a command the caller may not run the way the command does:
  unknown.** `HELP KICK` from an ordinary caller should not describe KICK
  when `KICK` itself answers "Unknown command". Use the same `allowed()`
  test `findCommand` uses. Same for guests and `PROFILE`, `PASSWORD`,
  `MAIL`.
- **`HELP CHAT` and `HELP MAIL` collide with menu names.** `menuFromText`
  accepts `chat` and `mail` as the Chat menu, and both are also commands
  with a long help entry here. Somebody has to decide which wins. The HELP
  entry is worded so it stays true either way, but one of the two will be
  unreachable by that spelling.
- **`/? p` and `/? /p` should both work.** The `/?` entry says `/? p`
  because it is fewer keystrokes on a C64; the table key is `/p`.
- **INFO's shortcut.** CLAUDE.md's naming table gives `INFO` the shortcut
  `I`. The 0.22.0 spec given to me did not. If `I` lands, the INFO block
  is already at 5 lines, so line 3 becomes
  `/i and /i3 in the room. I also works.` (37 columns).
- **MAIL is described as it is today.** If the mail subsystem queued for
  0.22.0 lands in the same build, the MAIL, /e and /email blocks need
  rewriting against it.

### Behaviour found while checking, which the text works around

- **`/t n +m` in the room has no permission check, and it is live.**
  `chat.cpp` calls `Bbs::cmdTimeAdjust` directly; `bbs.h` and the chat
  comment both say it "does its own permission check", and it does not
  (`bbs_sysop.cpp:573`). The shell's `TIME` checks `PERM_TIME` before
  calling it (`bbs_shell.cpp:1860`); the room does not. So any caller in
  the room can type `/t -1` and take themselves off the clock with no idle
  hangup, or `/t 3 +600` for somebody else. Present in the committed
  0.21.8, not only the working tree. The `/t` block describes the intended
  staff-only behaviour, not this.
- **Pages, broadcasts and SHUTDOWN warnings only reach a caller at the
  main prompt.** `deliverMail` requires `SState::Shell`
  (`bbs.cpp:2218`), so a caller in chat, files or forums gets nothing until
  they come out. The PAGE, /page and BROADCAST blocks say so. The comment in
  `serviceShutdown` says the bus "reaches a caller whatever they are doing:
  mid-form, inside the chat room" and that is not what the code does: a
  caller sitting in the room through a SHUTDOWN countdown sees no warning
  and is then hung up.
- **The room resets the bell every time somebody enters it.** `join()`
  sets `g_bell[slot] = true`. With BELL at the main prompt and `/b` meant
  to be one switch for the call, turning the bell off at the prompt and
  then typing CHAT would turn it back on. That line has to go when the
  setting moves.

### Where the docs and the source disagree

The source wins in the text above except where noted.

- **ESC in the room.** COMMANDS.md (CHAT row) says "`/q` or ESC leaves".
  The source clears the typed line and stays (`chat.cpp:2183`). CLAUDE.md's
  0.10.0 history says the same old thing, which is fine as history.
- **Squelch lasts the visit, not the call.** CHAT.md says a squelch lasts
  "until you hang up or type `/sq 3` again". `join()` clears `g_squelch`,
  so it ends when you leave the room.
- **The bell lasts the visit, not the call.** CHAT.md says `/b` is "for
  your call only". Same `join()` reset as above.
- **`/me` output.** CHAT.md shows `#1:Daytona) * waves`. The source
  prints `** Daytona waves **` and limits actions to 5 per 30 seconds,
  which CHAT.md does not mention.
- **`/page` reach.** CHAT.md and the chat-commands report describe it as
  reaching somebody wherever they are. It is delivered only at the main
  prompt, as above.
- **Mail replies.** CHAT.md says R "replies, on one line". `mailChoose`
  opens the full editor (`writeBegin`).
- **`mail_slots` default.** CHAT.md's settings example and the `chat.cpp`
  file header say 32; `kMailSlots` and the running default are 64. CHAT.md
  elsewhere says 64.
- **`FORUMS SCAN` is not staff only.** COMMANDS.md says staff only and the
  `cmdScan` comment says sysop only. Neither the command row (`CF_READ`)
  nor the handler checks anything, so any caller with the forums read level
  can run it, and it lists every forum including ones that caller may not
  read. Left out of the FORUMS block for that reason.
- **`CONFIG FORUMS TOPICS` does not exist.** `cmdScan` and COMMANDS.md
  both tell the sysop to type it. `CONFIG` takes one word; that line
  answers "No such page". `CONFIG forums` is the page.
- **No `BULLETIN` alias.** CLAUDE.md says FORUMS keeps a hidden `BULLETIN`
  alias. Nothing in `src/` defines one.
- **Node numbers.** COMMANDS.md says node arguments are `1`-`6` and the
  busy line starts "When all 6 nodes are in use". The source takes 1 to 10
  (`BBS_MAX_NODES`), plus `S` and `B`.
- **The sysop starts visible.** COMMANDS.md's HIDE row says "The sysop
  starts hidden". `elevate()` sets `visible = true` since 0.17.9.
- **`DESC` is gone.** COMMANDS.md still has a `DESC file text` row. No
  command table defines it; describing is `D` inside FILES.
- **`SYS` is any staff.** COMMANDS.md lists it under sysop screens; the row
  is `CF_STAFF`.
- **Stale queue item.** CLAUDE.md's queue still says FX and FILES both
  claim `F`. The source has fixed it: FX has no shortcut, FILES has `F`.
- **"There is no file transfer yet"** is still in COMMANDS.md's files
  section. XMODEM and YMODEM have shipped.
- Two counting slips, harmless: COMMANDS.md says "Four plugins ship" and
  lists five (forums not among them); CHAT.md says staff get "four more
  commands" and lists five.
- **The example in the brief.** The WHO sample given with this task said
  "With a number, that node's line refreshes". `WHO n` redraws the whole
  list every n seconds; n is seconds, not a node. The WHO block says that.

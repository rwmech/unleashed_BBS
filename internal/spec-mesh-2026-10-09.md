# The self-healing mesh: specification (2026-10-09)

A specification, written before any of it is built, the way LINK.md was.
No code. It extends the link (LINK.md) and replaces two earlier designs: the
one-hop relay in `internal/sat-types-2026-09-27.md` §1 and the hand-planted
chain in `internal/spec-callin-node-2026-10-05.md` §7 (`rep_up`, `rep_hops`).
Everything here is a decision with its reason. Where a figure was read off
the source it names the file; where it is an estimate it says so; where it
is unknown it says what would measure it.

What is settled and not reopened here (Rob, 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-09): a
full self-healing mesh, not planned routes and not the one-hop relay; our
own, over the link we have, not ESP-MESH-LITE; sats route and the board does
not forward; a repeater holds no key for what it carries; caller sessions
(CALLIN, family 3) ride it as well as cameras and doors; a reroute mid-call
is an accepted cost.

## The answer first

1. **A tree rooted at the board, with the routes computed by the board.**
   This is RPL's non-storing mode in shape (RFC 6550, RFC 6554): repeaters
   forward and fail over locally, the root alone holds the topology and
   source-routes everything sent down. The board computes; it never
   forwards a frame between two sats (Rule no. 1, and LINK.md's "a peer
   never talks to another peer through the link" stands).
2. **No repeater ever believes another repeater about a route.** Every piece
   of routing information travels sealed end to end, under the reporting
   node's own pairing, to the board, which is the only reader. Route
   instructions go back sealed to each node. So route authentication needs
   **no new key material at all**: no group key, no signatures, no
   certificates. It reuses the pairing every node already has with the
   board. This is the answer to the central question (§2).
3. **A link is used only when both ends report it**, in two independently
   sealed reports, and its cost uses the weaker of the two measurements. An
   outsider can spoof a MAC but cannot write a member's report, so it can
   make a link look worse and never better.
4. **The mesh is a layer under the engine, not inside it.** It implements the
   engine's `Io` (link.h:155): the engine keeps seeing peers by MAC, opening
   with their keys, exactly as now. The wrapper is added and removed below
   it. `src/core/link.*` changes in two places only (§14).
5. **Frames are never flooded.** Data goes up a parent chain and down a
   board-chosen source route, unicast, hop by hop. Even joining and pairing
   go up a repeater's own route as a proxied frame rather than by flooding.
   The shouting match (three boxes passing on everything they hear) cannot
   happen because nothing is passed on to "everyone".
6. **Wrapper: 3 to 7 bytes up, 3 to 6 down.** A meshed peer's sealed payload
   is **215 bytes**, one figure for every path length, so a reroute onto a
   longer path never makes a queued fragment too big (§3).
7. **At most 4 radio hops on the wire (3 repeaters), 3 by default.** The
   bench raises the default only if the latency measurement in §16 allows.
8. **Self-healing in three tiers**: the frame that finds a dead parent takes
   the next parent (milliseconds, local); the board answers a PATH_ERR by
   routing down through the dead link's alternate (one end-to-end retry);
   the full recompute follows (seconds). Every state a node can be in has a
   timed end (§8).
9. **No session is pinned to a path.** A pinned path that dies kills the
   session, which is the opposite of what a self-healing mesh is for.
   Instead the board does not move a working path that is carrying a
   session for a merely better one (soft pinning, §10).
10. **One root per mesh.** A repeater belongs to one board's mesh. A sat
    shared by several boards (LINK.md, "One satellite, several boards")
    reaches the others directly or not at all in this version (§1.4).
11. **The board's pairings must grow, and the "one pairing rather than
    thirty" reading is wrong.** With keys end to end the board holds a key
    for every sat behind every repeater. What stops being the wall is the
    ESP-NOW peer table (20), because far sats are never radio peers of the
    board. Pairings go to 32 when the mesh is on: heap, not static DRAM
    (§5.1).

## 0. What a mesh buys, and what it costs on one channel

Said first because the word "mesh" invites the wrong expectation.

**A mesh here buys reach and survivability, never capacity.** Every ESP-NOW
node sits on the board's router channel (LINK.md, Channels): the board is a
station and must be on its AP's channel, and every peer follows the board.
Zigbee spreads its hops across 16 channels; we have one. So:

- **Every hop is the same air again.** A frame that crosses three repeaters
  is transmitted four times on one channel, and a repeater cannot transmit
  while a neighbour in its range does. Reach goes up with the hop count;
  the capacity the channel has for everybody goes down with it.
- **All traffic converges on the board.** Every frame in a single-root tree
  ends at the board, so the last hop into the board carries the sum of the
  whole mesh. Adding repeaters at the edge adds no capacity at the centre.
- **That air is also the board's own Wi-Fi.** The router channel carries the
  callers' telnet and SSH. LINK.md already records the board's gateway pings
  rising from 1-4 ms to 7-21 ms when the link ran at 1 Mbps. Mesh airtime is
  therefore a Rule no. 1 question, not only a mesh question, and §16 gates
  it on the board's own gateway latency.
- **A few dozen boxes buy coverage, not callers.** The callin spec's §8 says
  the same from the other end: ten caller lines a board, whatever feeds them.

## 1. Shape: a tree rooted at the board

### 1.1 Roles

| Role | Who | Forwards | Holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **root** | the board | **never** | every member's pairing, every member's last report, the plan |
| **repeater** | a sat that says it can repeat (a role, §1.2) | other members' frames, sealed, unopened | its own pairing with the board, its neighbour table, its parent set and children list from the plan |
| **leaf** | any member that does not repeat | nothing | its own pairing, its neighbour table, its parent set |

A **member** is a sat paired with the board (an ordinary LINK.md pairing,
`k_link`, `k_sess`). Repeaters are members too: a repeater needs its own
session with the board for its reports and its plan. "Holds no key" means no
key for the traffic it forwards, which is exactly what sat-types §1 already
said ("R pairs with B as well, under its own key ... that key covers only
R's own messages").

### 1.2 Repeating is a role, not a kind

The callin spec settled "repeater" as one of the gateway sat's roles
(access point, terminal server, repeater), switched at run time. This spec
generalises it: **any member may repeat if it says so**, and the board
decides whether to use it. The camera sat could repeat; the gateway sat
usually will.

- **A sat that sleeps never repeats.** Zigbee's shape: mains-powered
  routers relay, end devices do not. A camsat in deep sleep between shots
  cannot carry anybody's keystrokes. A member reports `roles` in its sealed
  NEIGH_REPORT (§6): bit 0 can repeat, bit 1 stays awake, bit 2 on mains or
  a supply the sysop says is permanent. The board plans repeaters only
  from members with bits 0 and 1 set.
- **Not in HELLO.** HELLO's tag is computed over exactly 39 bytes and sits
  at offset 39 (`kHelloLen`, link.cpp:265 and 1119-1123), so a field cannot
  be added to HELLO without breaking every paired device. Roles go in the
  sealed report instead, which is also where the board wants them.

### 1.3 Why the board computes the routes

The settled rule is that the board does not route. What that rule protects
is the loop: no frame of anybody else's may cost the board per-frame work.
**Computing routes is not forwarding**, and this spec keeps the two apart:

- The board receives reports a few times a minute per node and recomputes
  at most once every 2 s (§5.4). That is a bounded job on the background
  runner, of the same class as the pairing arithmetic the runner already
  does (LINK.md, "Where the work runs"), not per-frame work.
- **It is the only place the answer to §2 exists.** Only the board holds a
  key with every member, so only the board can authenticate what every
  member says. A distributed routing protocol would need nodes to verify
  each other, which needs a group key or signatures (§2.2).
- **It adds no single point of failure.** Every path ends at the board;
  a mesh whose board is down carries nothing anyway.
- **A repeater still routes, in the sense that matters for Rule no. 1**: it
  picks the next hop for every frame, fails over to another parent on its
  own, and reports a dead link. The data plane is the sats'. The board only
  draws the map.

If a later bench shows the computation itself costs the loop anything, the
answer is to move it, not to distribute it (§16 item 9 measures it).

### 1.4 One root per mesh

A repeater belongs to exactly one board's mesh, named by that board's MAC in
its beacons (§4.1). A sat shared by several boards (up to 5, `kHosts`,
link.h:295) keeps its direct pairings with all of them, but only one board
may route through it, and a sat behind a repeater reaches only that
repeater's board.

The reason is the 1.2.0 multi-board lessons: every "state for the peer"
became "state per peer", and a window that served somebody new killed every
other board's sessions. A repeater with several roots holds several parent
sets, several plans, several rank freshnesses and several children lists,
and doubles the test matrix. Build one root well first. Nothing in the
wrapper format prevents a later version adding a root field.

### 1.5 Size

- **32 members a mesh** (`kMeshMax`), ids 1 to 32 on the wire (one byte,
  0 is the board, 255 is "none"). Bounded by the board's heap (§5.1) and by
  what the plan computation is measured to cost (§16), not by the format,
  which reaches 254.
- **12 children a repeater, and 12 for the board.** A node's ESP-NOW peer
  table has 20 entries (`ESP_NOW_MAX_TOTAL_PEER_NUM`). A repeater needs a
  radio peer for each node it sends unicast to: up to 3 parents and its
  children. 3 + 12 + the broadcast address = 16, which leaves 4 for the
  direct pairings a shared sat holds with other boards. The board enforces
  12 in the plan; a node never takes a child the plan did not give it.
- **4 radio hops at most** on the wire, so 3 repeaters (§3, §11).

## 2. Trust: how a route is authenticated

### 2.1 The problem

The payload is sealed end to end (AES-128-CCM, the 20-byte header as
associated data; LINK.md, Frames), so a repeater cannot read, alter or
replay what it carries. **Routing metadata lives outside that envelope by
construction**: a repeater has to read where a frame is going, and a
routing protocol has to tell nodes which way to send. In the one-hop relay
and the planted chain a human chose the path. In a self-healing mesh the
network chooses it, and then anything in radio range that can make itself
look like a good next hop can pull traffic through itself. It cannot read
what it attracts. It can drop it, delay it, and see who is talking and how
much.

### 2.2 What existing protocols do

| Protocol | How routing is protected | What it does not stop |
|---|---|---|
| **Zigbee** | one 128-bit **network key** held by every device secures the network layer, routing included; link keys are pairwise and only for the application layer | anybody who extracts the network key from one device (a physical attack on a field device is documented) is a full member and can forge routing for the whole network |
| **Babel** (RFC 8967) | an HMAC on every packet, under keys shared by the routers on a link | outsiders only. Every holder of the key may announce any route. RFC 8968 (DTLS) adds per-neighbour channels and still trusts any authenticated neighbour's routes |
| **RPL** (RFC 6550) | three modes: unsecured (lean on the link layer), preinstalled (a shared key on control messages), authenticated (preinstalled keys admit only leaves; routers fetch other keys from an authority, details left to a later specification that, as far as the papers found, was never written) | in practice deployments run unsecured mode over 802.15.4 link-layer security; research finds preinstalled mode open to insiders for want of per-sender authentication |
| **batman-adv** | none of its own: originator messages are unauthenticated, and the project's answer is to secure the links underneath (802.11s with SAE) | an authenticated node can still claim a perfect route |
| **Espressif `esp-now` component** | flooding with `forward_ttl` and an RSSI floor | not a trust mechanism at all |
| **RPL non-storing mode** (RFC 6550 §9, RFC 6554) | not a security feature, but the shape: nodes report their parents to the root (DAO), the root holds the topology and puts the full route in each downward packet | |

The pattern across all of them: **a group key keeps outsiders out and does
nothing about a member that lies**, and the one design that has an answer
for members (RPL's authenticated mode) needed an authority that hands out
keys, and never shipped.

### 2.3 What fits a cheap ESP32 paired with a four-digit code

- **A group key (the Zigbee and Babel shape): rejected.** Every repeater is
  a cheap board on a pole or in a barn, and reading a key out of an
  unencrypted ESP32's flash takes a USB cable. One extracted key forges
  routing for the whole mesh. Revoking a member means generating a new key
  and handing it to every other member through a mesh that may be under the
  attack it is being revoked for. It would also be a new long-term secret
  with no physical act behind it, where every key today comes from a
  pairing a sysop stood beside.
- **Signatures (an ECDSA certificate per node, signed by the board):
  rejected.** A P-256 signature is 64 bytes and a certificate with a public
  key about 140, against a 250-byte frame; a neighbour would verify one per
  beacon. ECDSA verification time on an ESP32 has not been measured here and
  would sit in a repeater's forwarding path. The only reason to pay it is to
  let nodes verify each other, and §2.4 removes that need.
- **The board as the only reader: adopted.** Each member already holds a
  key that only it and the board know, made by an ECDH exchange two sysops
  confirmed. If routing information only ever flows member to board and
  board to member, sealed under that key, then nothing about routing is
  ever believed from the air. This is RPL non-storing mode with its control
  plane sealed end to end instead of hop by hop.

### 2.4 The rules

- **T1. Routing information is believed only by the board, and only when
  sealed.** A member's neighbour report (NEIGH_REPORT), path error
  (PATH_ERR) and counters (MESH_STATS) are sealed session-0 messages under
  its `k_sess`. Anything else on the air about topology is a hint.
- **T2. A member believes only the board about its own place.** Its rank,
  its parents and its children arrive in a PLAN sealed under its own
  `k_sess`. A node never derives its rank from a neighbour's beacon, so
  there is no rank to poison and no count to infinity (§7).
- **T3. A link exists only when both ends report it.** A reports hearing X,
  X reports hearing A, each sealed under its own key. Cost uses the worse of
  the two RSSI figures and the worse of the two delivery figures (§5.3).
  The measurements are taken by each receiving radio, not claimed by the
  sender, so a spoofer near A can neither raise X's figure for A nor write
  X's report.
- **T4. A path is proved by end-to-end acknowledgement, and by nothing
  else.** A node that sends up a parent and gets no sealed answer from the
  board in time treats that parent as failed, whatever the parent's MAC
  acknowledgements and beacons say. A MAC ACK proves a radio heard the frame
  (LINK.md already refuses to trust one for the channel); it proves nothing
  about the frame reaching the board.
- **T5. A beacon is a hint, and a hint that fails is remembered.** A clear
  MESH_BEACON may lead an orphan to try a neighbour. If that neighbour does
  not produce an authenticated board answer within 1 s, it is not tried
  again for 60 s.
- **T6. Wrappers are not authenticated, and need not be.** Anything a
  repeater can do by rewriting a wrapper (send a frame the wrong way, cut
  its hop count, forge its origin id) ends in a frame that does not open at
  its destination or never arrives. That is the power to drop, which a
  repeater has anyway. A forged origin id makes the board try the wrong key
  and drop the frame as it drops a forged MAC today.
- **T7. Revocation is forgetting one pairing.** No group key, so nothing to
  rotate: `LINK FORGET n` on the board ends a member's trust everywhere,
  because nobody else ever trusted it about anything.
- **T8. Proxied pairing must be checked.** LINK.md lets a sysop skip the
  code comparison ("unchecked"), trusting that nobody else answered inside a
  window they stood beside. Through a mesh the window spans the mesh's whole
  area and every repeater sits in the exchange, so a pairing that arrives
  proxied (§9) is not offered unchecked: the sysop answers the code question
  or the pairing is not stored. The code is what rules out a repeater
  swapping public keys (sat-types §1).

### 2.5 What an attacker can do, and what limits it

| Attacker | Can | Cannot | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outsider in range** (no pairing) | spoof a member's MAC and answer frames sent to it at the MAC layer, then drop them; send beacons claiming our root and a low rank; send junk to repeaters asking for proxying; jam | read, alter or replay any frame; write any report the board believes; make a link look better than both ends measure it | T3, T4, T5; proxy rate limits (§9); every drop counted by reason |
| **Captured member** (its flash read) | everything an outsider can, plus report false neighbours and drop or delay what it carries; see which members are active, how much, and when | read anybody else's traffic (no key for it); make another member report a link to it; attract traffic over a link that does not physically exist | T3: it can lie only about its own half of a link. Delivery accounting (§5.3) prices a lossy repeater out of routes; LINK flags it; T7 removes it |
| **Wormhole** (an outsider tunnels real frames between two distant places, so two members genuinely hear each other through it) | create a link both ends really measure and report, then drop what crosses it | anything a captured member cannot | delivery accounting. This is the attack packet leashes (Hu, Perrig, Johnson, 2003) were invented for; leashes need tight clocks or positions we do not have, so we price loss rather than prevent the link |
| **Anybody listening** | see keystroke timing: a CALLIN keystroke is one frame | read keystrokes | nothing new: a radio listener on today's direct link sees the same timing. Keystroke timing leaks information (Song, Wagner, Tian, 2001, about SSH); the mesh adds more places to listen from, not a new leak. The site's copy should say so where it says the link is encrypted |

**What the design cannot stop, said plainly**: a repeater that carries a
path can always drop or delay it, which is the same as switching it off.
The mesh's answer is to notice and route around, and the sysop's is to
revoke. No routing protocol on a shared channel has a better one.

## 3. The wrapper

### 3.1 Telling a wrapped frame from a plain one

A plain frame's first byte is `ver` = 1 (LINK.md, Frames). A wrapped frame's
first byte is a wrapper type with bit 7 set. A board or sat built before the
mesh reads 0x8x as an unknown version and drops it, counted, which is the
right behaviour for a device that cannot take part. A meshed device still
takes plain frames from direct peers, unchanged.

| First byte | Wrapper | Sent by | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x01 | none | anybody, one radio hop | a plain frame, as today |
| 0x81 | **UP** | a member with an id, and each repeater on the way | a frame for the board |
| 0x82 | **DOWN** | the board, and each repeater on the way | a frame for one member, source-routed |
| 0x83 | **PROXY_UP** | the first repeater that heard it | a clear frame from a device the mesh has no id for (HELLO, the pairing steps) |
| 0x84 | **PROXY_DOWN** | the board, and each repeater on the way | the board's answer to such a device, delivered to its MAC on the last hop |

Every other value with bit 7 set is reserved and dropped.

### 3.2 Layouts

All one-byte fields; no field crosses a byte boundary, so there is no
endianness to get wrong.

**UP (0x81), 4 to 7 bytes**

```
 off  field
   0  0x81
   1  ctl     bits 0-2 ttl (hops left), bits 3-4 npath (0-3), bits 5-7 zero
   2  origin  the member's id, from its PLAN
   3  srank   the rank of the node that sent THIS hop (rewritten each hop)
   4  path    npath ids, the repeaters crossed so far, appended in order
```

**DOWN (0x82), 3 to 6 bytes**

```
 off  field
   0  0x82
   1  ctl     bits 0-1 nroute - 1 (1 to 4), bits 2-3 next (index into route), bits 4-6 ttl
   2  route   nroute ids: repeaters in order, the destination member last
```

**PROXY_UP (0x83), 9 to 12 bytes**

```
 off  field
   0  0x83
   1  ctl     bits 0-2 ttl, bits 3-4 npath (1-3: the first repeater is always path[0])
   2  mac     the device's MAC, 6 bytes, as the first repeater's radio received it
   8  rssi    i8, the device as the first repeater heard it
   9  path    npath ids
```

**PROXY_DOWN (0x84), 9 to 11 bytes**

```
 off  field
   0  0x84
   1  ctl     bits 0-1 nrep - 1 (1 to 3 repeaters), bits 2-3 next, bits 4-6 ttl
   2  route   nrep repeater ids
 2+nrep mac   the device's MAC, 6 bytes: the last repeater sends to it
```

**A repeater never touches the frame after the wrapper.** It rewrites `ctl`
and `srank`, appends to `path`, and copies the frame bytes as received.

### 3.3 Payload sizes

| | Bytes | Sealed payload |
|---|---|---|
| ESP-NOW frame | 250 (`ESP_NOW_MAX_DATA_LEN`, link.h:70) | |
| header + tag | 28 | 222 direct (`kPayloadMax`, link.h:73) |
| largest data wrapper (UP with 3 path ids) | 7 | **215** |
| largest clear frame proxied (PAIR_HELLO: 20 + 95) | 115 + 12 | fits, 123 bytes to spare |

**Decision: every peer uses 215 while the mesh is on, direct or not.** A
bulk message is cut into fragments when it is queued, and `len` and `nfrag`
are inside the authenticated header, so no repeater can re-cut a fragment
later. A peer that starts direct and is rerouted through two repeaters mid
picture would otherwise hold fragments too big for its new path. One figure
for every path costs 3.2% of bulk throughput and removes the case. With the
mesh off, 222 as today.

The callin spec's per-path table (222, 220, 219) is superseded: its wrapper
named one hop by index in the relay's own table, which cannot survive a
second hop or a reroute. The global id costs one byte and works at any
depth.

## 4. Neighbours and beacons

### 4.1 MESH_BEACON (family 0, type 26, clear)

Sent by a node with bits 0 and 1 of `roles` set (§1.2) and an id, broadcast
every 10 s with up to 2 s of random jitter, and once at once when its rank
changes (at most once a second). Also sent **unicast, in answer to a
DISCOVER**, at most once a second to any one MAC: that is how a node
sweeping channels finds a repeater in 20 ms of dwell (LINK.md's sweep,
`kDwellMs`, link.h:310) instead of waiting out a 10 s beacon.

```
 off size field
   0   6  root     the board's MAC: which mesh this is
   6   1  rank     hops to the board in its current plan; 0xFF "cut off"
   7   1  chan     the channel it believes the board is on
   8   1  free     children it can still take (12 less its count)
   9   1  roles
```

- **A rank is advertised only while it is fresh.** A node that has not
  received a sealed frame from the board for 15 s beacons rank 0xFF. This is
  what stops a cut-off subtree attracting orphans into itself after a
  router hop (§8.5).
- A beacon is clear and believed by nobody about anything (T5). Its sender's
  MAC and the RSSI the receiver measured are the useful part.
- `clearType` (link.h:116) gains this one type. It grants nothing.

### 4.2 The neighbour table, on every member

One entry for each MAC heard sending a MESH_BEACON for this root (or any
mesh frame) in the last 35 s (three and a half beacons).

| Field | Bytes | |
|---|---|---|
| mac | 6 | |
| id | 1 | from its beacon's PLAN-assigned id, 255 if unknown |
| rank | 1 | as it last beaconed: a hint |
| rssi | 1 | i8, an exponential average, 1/4 new |
| heardAt | 4 | `plat::since` against it, never subtraction (§15) |
| sentOk, sentFail | 2 + 2 | MAC acknowledgements of unicasts to it, halved when either passes 1,000 |
| failRun | 1 | send callbacks in a row that said FAIL |
| rate | 1 | 24 Mbps or the 1 Mbps fallback, **its own**, per neighbour (§15) |
| triedAt | 4 | when T5 last ruled it out, 0 for never |
| flags | 1 | parent, child, suspect |
| **total** | **24** | 20 entries: **480 bytes**, heap, allocated when the mesh starts |

When the table is full, the entry heard longest ago that is not a parent or
a child goes. A parent or child is never evicted by a stranger's beacons,
which is the cheap defence against a beacon flood displacing a working
path.

### 4.3 What a node reports

NEIGH_REPORT (§6) carries, for up to 20 neighbours: MAC, average RSSI and a
delivery byte (`sentOk * 255 / (sentOk + sentFail)`, 0xFF when nothing has
been sent to it). Sent when a neighbour appears or goes, when an RSSI moves
6 dB or more from what was last reported, or when a parent fails; at most
once every 5 s, and every 60 s regardless so the board's picture cannot go
stale silently.

## 5. The board: pairings, reports and the plan

### 5.1 Pairings, and the fan-out correction

The settled note says the board "holds one pairing rather than thirty". With
keys end to end that cannot be true: a sat's frames are sealed under a key
only it and the board hold, so the board needs that key, one per sat. What
the mesh does change:

- **Radio peers.** Today the engine registers every pairing as an ESP-NOW
  peer (`Io::addPeer`, link.h:177), and the ESP-NOW table holds 20. In the
  mesh the board's radio peers are only its own children (12 at most), and
  `addPeer` for a far member is a no-op in the mesh layer. So the 20-entry
  table stops being the wall for the number of sats.
- **Pairings grow to 32 when the mesh is on.** `struct Engine::Peer` is
  **240 bytes** on the host build (read off the compiler, 2026-10-09; the
  ESP32 figure is to be read off the target ELF, expected the same as no
  field is pointer-sized). 32 pairings is 7,680 bytes against today's 8 at
  1,920: **+5,760 bytes of heap, and no static DRAM**, from PSRAM where the
  board has it, as the engine already is (LINK.md, Budgets). The callin
  spec's figure for raising `kPeers` to 20 (about 2.9 KB) is the same
  arithmetic.
- **The pairings file gains an id column** (version 3,
  `#link-peers 3`): the member's mesh id, kept across reboots so a
  restart does not renumber the mesh and invalidate every path held in
  flight.

### 5.2 What the board keeps per member

On the heap, beside the pairings:

| | Bytes |
|---|---|
| last report: up to 12 neighbours (id, rssi, delivery) | 36 |
| roles, rank, plan version sent, last report time, flags | 8 |
| parent set (3 ids), children count | 4 |
| delivery accounting: frames expected and delivered over routes through it | 8 |
| **per member** | **56**, so **1,792** for 32 |

The report on the wire carries 20 neighbours by MAC; the board keeps the 12
best that are members (it can map a MAC to an id only for its own members,
and a neighbour that is not a member is useful to nobody's route).

**Board mesh heap in all**: about 1.8 KB of graph plus 5.8 KB of extra
pairings, **about 7.6 KB**, an estimate from the struct sizes above. The
link already takes about 19 KB while on (LINK.md, Budgets). Measured in §16.

### 5.3 Link cost

For a link A to X, used only when both A's and X's latest reports list each
other (T3):

- `rssi = min(A's figure for X, X's figure for A)`
- `deliv = min(A's delivery to X, X's delivery to A)`, ignoring 0xFF; both
  unknown means a link nobody has used yet, priced from RSSI alone.
- `cost = 1 + rssiPenalty(rssi) + lossPenalty(deliv)`, where 1 is the hop
  itself, so among equally good links fewer hops win.

The penalty functions are deliberately not given numbers here: they are
fitted to the bench's RSSI-against-loss measurements (§16 item 4), not
guessed. Only their shape is decided: flat above the RSSI where the bench
sees no loss, steep below it, and a delivery under a threshold is priced as
nearly unusable rather than as slightly worse.

**Delivery accounting.** The board knows the route of every frame it sends
down and the `path` of every frame it receives up. A frame sent down and
never acknowledged end to end counts as a loss against that route. A
repeater that sits on two or more lossy routes while routes avoiding it are
clean gets a cost penalty on every link through it and is marked in LINK as
"loses frames". This is a heuristic and is described as one: attribution
across a multi-hop route is not exact, and the sysop's revoke is the real
remedy for a member that drops on purpose.

### 5.4 The plan

- **Computed on the runner**, as a `runner::Job` of the link plugin's, never
  on the loop. Input: the members that report, their confirmed links and
  costs. Output, per member: a rank (its hop count from the board along its
  chosen path) and an ordered parent set of up to 3, each parent of a
  **strictly lower rank** (§7); per repeater, its children with their MACs.
- **Algorithm**: a shortest-path tree from the board over confirmed links,
  using only repeat-capable members as interior nodes, rejecting any path
  longer than the hop cap, and giving no node more than 12 children. The
  second and third parents are the next-cheapest neighbours of lower rank.
  At 32 members and at most 12 links each this is a few hundred edge
  relaxations; its time on the ESP32 is unmeasured and §16 measures it.
- **When**: at most once every 2 s, and only when an input changed: a
  member up or down, a report that moved a link's cost by a quarter or more,
  a PATH_ERR, a pairing added or forgotten.
- **Hysteresis**: a member's parent set is changed only when a parent
  failed, or when a new path is at least 25% cheaper. Fluctuating RSSI
  then moves nothing.
- **Plan version**: a 16-bit counter, incremented on every change and
  compared with `plat::since`-style wrap-safe arithmetic (a 16-bit
  difference, not a magnitude), so it never needs resetting.
- **Distribution**: a PLAN (§6) to each member whose plan changed, sealed,
  reliable, on session 0. A parent learns about a new child from its own
  PLAN, so it never accepts a child the board did not give it.

## 7. Loop control

Three independent mechanisms, because a loop on one channel takes the air
from the board's own callers and is therefore a Rule no. 1 failure, not
just a mesh fault.

1. **Ranks are strictly decreasing toward the board, by construction.**
   Every parent the board gives a node has a lower rank than the node, so
   upward forwarding along any parent, first or third, always approaches
   the board. Downward routes are the board's own source routes, so they
   cannot loop at all unless the board's plan is stale.
2. **Data-path rank check** (RPL's data-path validation, RFC 6550 §11.2):
   every UP frame carries `srank`, the rank of the node that sent this hop.
   A repeater that receives an UP frame whose `srank` is not greater than
   its own rank is looking at a frame that is going the wrong way: two
   nodes hold plans that disagree. It drops the frame, counts it, and sends
   PATH_ERR reason 4; the board re-sends both plans. This catches the one
   transient a plan change can make (node A on an old plan forwarding to B
   while B, on the new one, now has A as its parent).
3. **TTL.** UP and DOWN carry a hop count of at most 4 and every repeater
   decrements it; at 0 the frame is dropped and counted. Not the main loop
   defence, the last one: it bounds the airtime any fault can spend.

**No count to infinity.** Distance-vector protocols loop because a node
learns its distance from a neighbour that learned it from the node. Here no
node learns its rank from anybody but the board (T2), and a cut-off node
advertises 0xFF (§4.1), so there is no chain of neighbours believing each
other.

**Nothing floods.** The only broadcasts are MESH_BEACON (never forwarded)
and the clear DISCOVER and PAIR_HELLO a device sends (never forwarded as
broadcasts: a repeater proxies them up its own route, §9). There is no
frame type a repeater rebroadcasts, so there is no duplicate cache to get
wrong.

## 8. Failure, detection and repair

### 8.1 Upward: the frame that finds the failure takes the new path

ESP-NOW's send callback reports per unicast whether the next radio
acknowledged at the MAC layer (LINK.md, The radio), after the MAC's own
retries. A node sending UP to parent p:

1. On FAIL it resends the same frame to p once at once, and once more at
   1 Mbps (the existing per-neighbour fallback, LINK.md, three MAC failures
   in a row, now counted per neighbour in the table).
2. If both fail, **the same frame goes to the next parent in the set**, and
   p is marked suspect. A keystroke in flight is delayed by the time of the
   failed sends, not by an end-to-end retry.
3. Three frames in a row that needed step 2 for p make p failed: it is
   skipped until a new PLAN, and the node sends NEIGH_REPORT with the
   urgent flag through the parent that worked.

Detection time is driven by traffic. A node carrying a CALLIN or door
session sends every keystroke and acknowledgement up its path, so a dead
parent is found by the next keystroke. An idle node sends PING every 5 s
(`kPingMs`, link.h:307) and finds out by its next PING. An idle node with a
dead parent loses nothing by finding out late.

T4 covers the case the MAC cannot: a parent that acknowledges and drops (a
captured member, or a parent cut off from the board). A node that sees its
end-to-end retries (LINK.md, 150 ms doubling) reach their third try on
session 0 or on any session, while its parent's MAC acknowledgements say
fine, marks that parent failed exactly as in step 3.

### 8.2 Downward: the board takes the alternate at once

The board source-routes each DOWN frame along the chain of first parents.
A repeater that cannot reach the next id on the route (its own step 1
fails, or the id is not its child) drops the frame and sends PATH_ERR with
the id it lost.

The board, without waiting for a recompute, routes that member's next
frames down through its **second parent**: it holds every member's parent
set, so the alternate is a table lookup. The lost frame itself is resent
by the engine's ordinary end-to-end retry (first after 150 ms), which by
then takes the new route. A recompute follows on its 2 s schedule.

### 8.3 The node's states, every one with an end

| State | Means | Leaves when | And at most |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PLANNED** | holds a fresh PLAN, sends via its parents | all parents failed: ORPHAN. No sealed board frame for 15 s: CUT_OFF | as long as it works |
| **CUT_OFF** | its parents may be up but the board is not answering through them | a sealed board frame arrives: PLANNED | 15 s, then ORPHAN |
| **ORPHAN** | no usable parent | a sealed board frame arrives through a neighbour it tried: PLANNED (with a temporary parent until the next PLAN) | 30 s, then SCAN |
| **SCAN** | LINK.md's channel sweep, DISCOVER on each channel | a board BEACON (direct) or a member's MESH_BEACON (§4.1) on a channel, followed by an authenticated board answer: PLANNED | one full sweep, about a quarter of a second, then ORPHAN on the last good channel and round again |
| **JOINING** | after boot or pairing: HELLO sent through a proxy | HELLO_ACK and PLAN arrive: PLANNED | 5 s per try, then ORPHAN |

In ORPHAN a node tries the neighbours it can hear, best beaconed rank
first, one at a time: an urgent NEIGH_REPORT, UP-wrapped, sent to that
neighbour as if it were a parent. A repeater forwards an UP frame from a
member that is not its child (it is sealed and harmless) at no more than 2
a second for any one origin, so an orphan can always get one report
through. A neighbour that produces no sealed board answer within 1 s is
ruled out for 60 s (T5). Every one of these waits is measured with
`plat::since` and has its own end; there is no state a node can sit in
indefinitely waiting for something that will not come. The 1.2.0 pairing
that wedged a satellite for good is the shape this table exists to rule
out.

### 8.4 A repeater going away

Its children each find out by §8.1 and move to their next parents; a child
with none goes ORPHAN. The board finds out by the PATH_ERRs, by the
children's urgent reports, and finally by the repeater's own missed PINGs
(`kHostQuietMs`, 20 s, link.h:309, which is too slow to be the mechanism,
so it is the backstop). Nothing waits on the last.

### 8.5 The router changing channel

The board moves with its router; every member is left on the old channel,
and nobody on the old channel can hear the board. With the freshness rule
(§4.1) the whole old-channel mesh beacons 0xFF within 15 s, so no orphan
reattaches into a subtree that is cut off, and every member reaches SCAN.
The board's direct children find the board by LINK.md's sweep, get a PLAN,
and beacon a fresh rank at once and answer DISCOVERs; the next layer finds
them in its own sweep. The mesh re-forms one layer at a time, each layer
in about the time of one sweep plus one HELLO round trip.

The total time for a three-layer mesh to re-form is not known and is the
single most important number §16 measures (item 6). Until it is measured,
the honest statement to a sysop is "a router that changes channel takes the
far sats offline for some seconds, more the further out they are".

## 9. Joining and pairing through the mesh

### 9.1 Proxying, instead of flooding

A device that has no id (after a boot, or not yet paired) sends its clear
frames (HELLO, PAIR_HELLO, PAIR_NONCE) as it does today: HELLO unicast,
pairing frames broadcast. A repeater that hears one and may proxy it wraps
it in PROXY_UP and sends it **up its own route**, as if it were its own UP
frame. The board answers in PROXY_DOWN along the reverse of the `path`, and
the last repeater unicasts the clear answer to the device's MAC.

- **What a repeater proxies**: HELLO always; PAIR_HELLO, PAIR_NONCE and the
  sealed PAIR_ACK only while a PAIR_RELAY window is open (the board sends
  it to every repeater when the sysop types `LINK PAIR`, for the window's 2
  minutes). Nothing else is ever proxied.
- **Rate limits**: one proxied frame a second for any one device MAC, four
  a second in all. A frame over the limit is dropped and counted. This is
  what bounds an outsider using the mesh to carry junk to the board: the
  board checks a HELLO's HMAC tag (`helloTag`, link.cpp:1122) and drops one
  that fails, as it drops one arriving directly.
- **Several repeaters hear the same device**: the board receives the frame
  more than once, takes the first, and answers through the repeater whose
  PROXY_UP reported the strongest `rssi` among those that arrived within
  50 ms of the first.
- **Channel trust**: LINK.md's rule is that a BEACON is the only thing a
  peer trusts about the channel. A device behind repeaters never hears a
  board BEACON, so the rule becomes: **a device settles on a channel only
  when an authenticated board frame arrives on it** (a HELLO_ACK, whose tag
  only the board can make, or a sealed frame). A MESH_BEACON is the hint
  that sends it looking.

### 9.2 Pairing a device the board cannot hear

The exchange is LINK.md's, unchanged byte for byte: commit, reveal, the
code. Only the transport differs. The 4-digit code is what makes it safe
with repeaters in the middle: a repeater that substituted its own public
keys would make the two codes differ (sat-types §1). Hence T8: a proxied
pairing is never stored unchecked.

### 9.3 Getting an id

The board assigns a member its id at pairing (lowest free, 1 to 32) and
keeps it in the pairings file. A member learns it from its first PLAN after
each HELLO. A member never chooses its own id.

## 10. A session across a reroute

- **Sessions are end to end and know nothing of paths.** A session belongs
  to a member's pairing (`Sess::peer`, link.cpp:313), not to a route, so a
  reroute changes nothing in any session's state.
- **Frames lost on the dead path are resent by the engine**, end to end, as
  any lost frame is today, and take the new path. Nothing is delivered
  twice (`seq`, LINK.md) and nothing is lost unless the retries run out.
- **Reordering during the switch is already handled**: a frame on the new,
  shorter path may overtake one on the old; the 64-wide replay window and
  per-session `seq` absorb it. The window is 64 packet numbers per
  direction; a path switch reorders a handful.
- **No hard pinning (decision).** A session pinned to a path dies with the
  path. **Soft pinning instead**: while a member carries a session of family
  2 or 3 (a door or a caller), the board does not apply the 25%-cheaper rule
  to it or to any repeater on its path. Those move only on failure. A
  picture in flight is not pinned: it is bulk, retried and resumed by the
  window, and moving it to a better path helps it.
- **What the caller sees.** A caller's terminal is on the gateway sat, so
  when the path to the board breaks, the board cannot tell the caller
  anything: there is no path. **The gateway sat must say it**: when its
  upstream has been unanswered for 2 s it shows its caller a line in the
  board's voice that it is finding another way, and removes nothing it
  holds. The wording is satwords' and Rob's; the 2 s rule is this spec's.
  For a door sat behind the mesh the board holds the caller, and LINK.md's
  existing "--> Lost the signal." path applies unchanged when retries run
  out.
- **When retries do run out.** The engine's 8 tries (150 ms doubling to
  1.2 s, counted only while the far end is heard, LINK.md) are about 7 s.
  A repair inside that is invisible except as a pause. One outside it ends
  the session as a lost signal does today. §16 item 5 measures which side
  of 7 s a real repair falls.

## 11. Latency

The bar is today's keystroke echo with five callers on the bench: **p50
4 ms, p95 12 ms** (telnet over the LAN). A routed CALLIN keystroke goes up
h radio hops, waits for the board's link tick, and its echo comes back down
h hops.

**Two figures go into it, and only one has been measured.**

- **f, the cost of one hop.** CLAUDE.md and the callin spec carry "2-6 ms a
  hop forwarding from the receive callback" and "about 40 ms on a tick".
  sat-types §1, which is where the first figure starts, calls it "a few
  milliseconds a hop, estimate, to measure". **It has not been measured on
  this hardware**, and this spec treats it as an estimate.
- **The board's link tick, which is measured, and may be the larger
  term.** The link plugin polls every 20 ms (LINK.md, Where the work runs).
  A keystroke that arrives just after a tick waits up to 20 ms for the loop
  to take it, even on today's direct link. On average 10.

Added echo, in ms, as `2 x h x f` plus the tick (worst 20):

| Radio hops h | f = 2 | f = 4 | f = 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (direct) | 4 + 20 = 24 | 8 + 20 = 28 | 12 + 20 = 32 |
| 2 (one repeater) | 8 + 20 = 28 | 16 + 20 = 36 | 24 + 20 = 44 |
| 3 (two repeaters) | 12 + 20 = 32 | 24 + 20 = 44 | 36 + 20 = 56 |
| 4 (three repeaters) | 16 + 20 = 36 | 32 + 20 = 52 | 48 + 20 = 68 |

These sit on top of the gateway's own hop to the caller, which the callin
spec found may be the biggest term of all (a phone in power save holding
downlink about 205 ms).

**Decisions:**

- **The mesh's own share of a CALLIN echo is held to p95 50 ms.** That is a
  target chosen here, about four times today's p95, not a perceptual
  measurement; Rob may move it. It is the yardstick for the hop cap, not a
  number to print anywhere.
- **The default cap is 3 radio hops (2 repeaters), the wire's is 4.** At
  f = 6 three hops is just over the line and four is well over; at f = 2
  four hops is fine. The bench decides whether 4 is allowed by default
  (§16 items 1 and 3). The callin spec's `rep_hops` 1..2 is the same
  default reached the other way.
- **Every hop forwards from a task woken by the receive callback, never
  from a tick** (§13). A repeater on a 20 ms tick would add 40 ms a hop
  round trip and spend the budget in one hop.
- **The board's 20 ms tick is the term to attack first**, before any hop
  is optimised, and it is outside this spec (§14 item 10).

## 12. Throughput and airtime

- **Bulk end to end is bounded by the window over the round trip**: 16
  fragments (64 with PSRAM) of 215 bytes in flight, per LINK.md. A longer
  path has a longer round trip, so throughput falls with hops even before
  airtime does.
- **Every hop is the same air** (§0). sat-types §1 estimated one hop at 30-45
  KB/s against 54-88 KB/s direct, an estimate. For more hops this spec gives
  no figure: it depends on which repeaters hear each other (a chain whose
  hops are all in each other's range shares one channel's airtime among all
  of them; one whose ends cannot hear each other can transmit at once, and
  then suffers hidden-terminal collisions). §16 item 4 measures it.
- **The board's callers pay for the mesh's airtime.** The board's
  gateway latency, measured with a picture crossing two repeaters, is a
  Rule no. 1 gate (§16 item 2), the same test that kept LR mode off by
  default.
- **Per hop, the existing rate rule holds per neighbour**: 24 Mbps, 1 Mbps
  after three MAC failures in a row, back after 30 s clean. A 1 Mbps hop in
  the middle of a path paces the whole path, so the link's cost (§5.3)
  includes the rate the hop is on.

## 13. Where the work runs

**On a repeater** (a sat; there is no BBS loop to protect, but there is a
caller if it is also a gateway):

| Where | What | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi task, receive callback | length check; the first byte: a wrapper for forwarding goes into the forwarding ring, anything for this node into its own rings, as today | one copy of up to 250 bytes |
| **forwarding task**, woken by a task notification from the callback, priority just under the Wi-Fi task | wrapper checks (ttl, rank, route index, rate limits), rewrite, pick the next hop, `esp_now_send` | per frame; the ring is 16 frames (about 4.2 KB); a full ring drops and counts |
| Wi-Fi task, send callback | the neighbour's ok and fail counts, the outstanding count, and for a forwarded frame that failed, a flag the forwarding task acts on (§8.1) | atomics |

Why a task and not the callback itself: LINK.md's own reading of the IDF is
"do not do lengthy operations in the callback function", and whether
`esp_now_send` may be called from the receive callback is not stated in the
5.3.1 documentation this project checked. A notification and a
high-priority task is the shape that is safe without that answer. Measuring
f (§16 item 1) measures this shape.

**On the board:**

| Where | What | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi task, receive callback | strip the wrapper, map the origin id to the member's MAC, hand the engine an ordinary frame through its two rings; a wrapper the board should never receive (DOWN, or UP not addressed to it) is dropped and counted | one copy |
| BBS loop, the link tick | unchanged: control frames, a few a second | LINK.md's figures |
| runner | bulk as today; the plan computation; the pairing arithmetic | one job at a time; the plan job measured in §16 |
| link tick, send side | wrap each frame for a far member in a DOWN wrapper from the member's current route: a table lookup and up to 6 bytes | per frame |

**The board never forwards.** A frame arriving at the board is for the
board or it is dropped. There is no path in the board's code that sends a
received frame on.

## 14. What the existing design has to change

Listed so nothing is discovered late. The engine (`src/core/link.*`, which
every sat builds) changes in items 1 and 2 only.

1. **`Io` gains `payloadMax()`**, default 222, which the mesh layer answers
   215 (§3.3). The engine's `kPayloadMax` (link.h:73) becomes the ceiling
   and fragment sizing reads the Io's figure.
2. **`Io::addPeer` is no longer "every pairing is a radio peer"**: the mesh
   layer registers only the board's children with ESP-NOW. The engine keeps
   calling it; the layer decides. (Nothing in the engine changes, but every
   reader of LINK.md's "Peers: 20 in the ESP-NOW table" sentence has to
   learn the difference between a pairing and a radio peer.)
3. **`BBS_LINK_PEERS` / `kPeers` 8 becomes 32 while the mesh is on**
   (link.h:294), heap only (§5.1).
4. **The pairings file goes to version 3** with an id column (§5.1). Version
   2 files still read, with ids assigned at load.
5. **Family 0 gains six types** (§6) and `clearType` one (MESH_BEACON).
   LINK.md's family 0 table gains six rows.
6. **The channel-trust rule is generalised** (§9.1): an authenticated board
   frame settles a channel; LINK.md's "BEACON is the only thing a peer
   trusts" stays true for direct peers.
7. **A peer's rescan rule moves down a level.** Today three missed PINGs
   start a channel sweep. In the mesh a node first works through its parent
   set and ORPHAN (§8.3); a sweep is the last resort. The sleeping sender's
   fast rescan (LINK.md, `setFastRescan`) does the same: its MAC failures
   go to its next parent first.
8. **Unchecked pairing is refused when proxied** (T8). LINK.md's
   "checked/unchecked" stays for direct pairings.
9. **The callin spec's §7 and its repeater page are superseded**: `rep_up`
   (a configured upstream) goes, because the board plans upstreams; `rep_hops`
   becomes the board's hop cap, a board setting in CONFIG link rather than a
   node's. sat-types §1's two-byte wrapper with a per-relay index is
   superseded by the global id (§3.3).
10. **Found, not this spec's to fix: the board's 20 ms link tick is the
    largest measured term in a CALLIN keystroke's echo**, mesh or no mesh
    (§11). Waking the loop when the control ring takes a frame, rather than
    on the tick, would take up to 20 ms off every keystroke on every path.
    It belongs to the CALLIN work, and it touches the loop's `select()`, so
    it is a design question for that lane.
11. **Found, not fixed: the "one pairing rather than thirty" note in
    CLAUDE.md and this job's brief is wrong as worded** (§5.1). It should
    read "one radio peer per neighbour rather than one per sat".
12. **The host radio needs a reach table** (sat-types §1 already asked for
    it): the host's UDP medium lets everybody hear everybody, so no host
    test can prove a frame went through a repeater. Before any mesh code,
    `host/test_link.cpp`'s medium needs "A cannot hear C".

| **Every state needs an end**: a pairing stopped part way wedged a satellite for good | the node states | §8.3's table: every state has a timed exit |
| **`#if` cannot see an enumerator** | build switches | the mesh is compiled into every image and off by default, like the link; any compile-time switch is a macro (`BBS_LINK_MESH`), never an enum, and a board.h `#error` catches a profile that sets it wrong |
| **A verifier needs its own proof**: a guard-removal runner reported success having tested nothing | the mesh tests | every guard in §16's host list is proved by removal, and the runner asserts how many cases it ran |
| **Proving a thing is refused is not proving which check refused it** | the loop checks (rank, ttl, route index) | each refusal is counted under its own reason, and each test asserts the reason, not only the drop |
| **A count written beside a table** (CONFIG ranges, `kAreaParts`) | 12 children, 3 parents, 20 neighbours, 32 members | each is one constant, and the wire format's field widths are `static_assert`ed against them |

## 16. Measured on hardware before any of it is trusted

A list to execute, in order; each item says what it settles. Boards: the
WROOM as the board (the floor), an ESP32-CAM (the static-DRAM floor, mesh
off and on), three or four plain ESP32 dev boards as repeaters and leaves,
and the bench's own access point, whose channel can be changed. Every
item reads SYS's loop average, loop worst and slow-pass count on the board
before and after, because Rule no. 1 is the gate on everything.

1. **f, the cost of one hop.** A leaf sends timestamped one-frame messages
   to the board through 0, 1, 2 and 3 repeaters; the board answers at once.
   p50 and p95 round trip at 24 Mbps and with every hop forced to 1 Mbps,
   at 16 and 215 bytes. Settles §11's table and whether the default cap is
   3 or 4.
2. **The board's callers' latency under mesh load.** The board's gateway
   ping and a telnet caller's echo (the bench's existing p50/p95 method)
   while a picture crosses two repeaters, and while four leaves PING. A
   regression against the same run without the mesh is a blocker.
3. **CALLIN echo end to end**, a terminal on a gateway sat at 1, 2 and 3
   hops, measured at the terminal: against the 50 ms mesh share (§11).
4. **RSSI against loss, and bulk throughput.** Per hop at distances and
   through walls: delivery against RSSI (fits §5.3's penalty functions),
   and a picture's KB/s at 1, 2 and 3 hops, all repeaters in mutual range
   and in a line where the ends cannot hear each other.
5. **Repair under a caller.** A repeater on a CALLIN caller's path loses
   power while the caller types steadily. Measured at the terminal: the
   pause (last echo before, first after), keys lost (must be 0), and
   whether repair finished inside the engine's 7 s of retries. Repeat with
   an alternate parent present and absent.
6. **A router channel change.** Change the AP's channel with a three-layer
   mesh up. The time for each layer to be PLANNED again, and the total.
   This is the number a sysop will ask for.
7. **An outsider.** A rogue node beaconing our root at rank 1; spoofed
   origin ids; a proxy flood at 50 frames a second. Each must be dropped
   and counted by reason, no member's path may move, and the board's loop
   figures must not.
8. **A captured member.** A repeater whose firmware drops 100%, then 30%,
   of what it forwards. The board must route around the first within one
   recompute and mark the second "loses frames"; record how long each
   takes.
9. **The plan computation.** Its runtime on the WROOM's runner at 4, 16 and
   32 members (synthetic reports are fine), and whether it shows in the
   loop's figures at all.
10. **Memory.** Board internal heap free and low with the mesh on and 32
    pairings, on the WROOM and the ESP32-CAM (PSRAM); static DRAM off the
    ELF of every env against the parent build (target: 0 bytes added).
11. **A soak.** Four members, one carrying a door caller, 24 hours, with
    the repeaters power-cycled on a timer: sessions lost, PATH_ERRs, plan
    changes, heap low on every node.

**Host tests, before any of the above** (with the reach table, §14 item
12): forwarding up and down through 1-3 repeaters; local failover; PATH_ERR
and the board's alternate; the rank check catching a deliberately stale
plan; TTL at 0; each orphan and cut-off timeout ending; a forged origin id
dropped as a tag failure; a beacon flood not evicting a parent; proxied
pairing refused unchecked; all with the clock from 0x90000000. Every guard
proved by removal; the runner asserts its case count.

## 17. Left open, for Rob

Not options shrugged at: these are the calls this spec cannot make for him.

- **The 50 ms target** for the mesh's share of a CALLIN echo (§11): it is
  this spec's number, not a measurement of anybody's perception.
- **32 members** (§1.5): set by heap and an unmeasured computation; the
  format reaches 254.
- **One root per mesh** (§1.4): a deliberate first-version limit. A
  fairground with four boards is four meshes on one channel, each sat in
  one of them.
- **Whether the site says the keystroke-timing sentence** (§2.5). It is true
  of the direct link today, so it is not a mesh question, but the mesh is
  where somebody will ask.

# The self-healing mesh: specification (2026-10-09, revision 2)

A specification, written before any of it is built, the way LINK.md was.
No code. It extends the link (LINK.md) and replaces two earlier designs: the
one-hop relay in `internal/sat-types-2026-09-27.md` §1 and the hand-planted
chain in `internal/spec-callin-node-2026-10-05.md` §7 (`rep_up`, `rep_hops`).
Everything here is a decision with its reason.

What is settled and not reopened here (Rob, 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-09): a
full self-healing mesh, not planned routes and not the one-hop relay; our
own, over the link we have, not ESP-MESH-LITE; a tree rooted at the board;
routing sealed end to end under the existing pairings; no flooding; no new
key material; sats route and the board does not forward; a repeater holds
no key for what it carries; caller sessions (CALLIN, family 3) ride it as
well as cameras and doors; a reroute mid-call is an accepted cost.

**Revision 2 (2026-10-09)** changes four things on review: two depth limits
instead of one, set by what each sat is for (§3), with a wrapper redesigned
so depth costs payload only where it must (§5); the one-root limit costed
against N meshes sharing a channel rather than deferred (§4); the mesh
reports its own health from what already arrives, with no extra airtime
(§15); and every number now says what kind of number it is (below).

### How to read the numbers

Every figure in this document is one of five kinds, and says which where it
appears. Nothing marked derived or chosen has been measured, and none of it
may be quoted as measured. §19 is the gate that turns them into
measurements.

| Mark | Means | Example |

| **read** | read off a header, the source or the compiler, exact | `ESP_NOW_MAX_DATA_LEN` 250 |
| **arithmetic** | exact consequence of the frame format | the wrapper is 2 + D bytes |

| **chosen** | a design value picked here, to be moved by measurement or by Rob | the 10 s beacon interval |

## The answer first

1. **A tree rooted at the board, with the routes computed by the board.**
   RPL's non-storing mode in shape (RFC 6550, RFC 6554): repeaters forward
   and fail over locally, the root alone holds the topology and
   source-routes everything sent down. The board computes; it never
   forwards a frame between two sats (Rule no. 1, and LINK.md's "a peer
   never talks to another peer through the link" stands).
2. **No repeater ever believes another repeater about a route.** Every
   piece of routing information travels sealed end to end, under the
   reporting node's own pairing, to the board, the only reader. Route
   instructions go back sealed to each node. So route authentication needs
   **no new key material**: it reuses the pairing every node already has
   with the board (§2).
3. **A link is used only when both ends report it**, in two independently
   sealed reports, and its cost uses the weaker of the two measurements. An
   outsider can make a link look worse and never better.
4. **The mesh is a layer under the engine, not inside it.** It implements
   the engine's `Io` (link.h:155); the engine keeps seeing peers by MAC and
   opening with their keys, as now.
5. **Frames are never flooded.** Data goes up a parent chain and down a
   board-chosen source route; joining and pairing go up a repeater's own
   route as a proxied frame.
6. **Two depth limits, not one** (§3). An **interactive depth** for members
   that carry callers (door sats, gateway sats), governed by the latency
   budget, chosen default 3 radio hops. A **data depth** for everything
   store-and-forward (pictures, sensors, timelapse), governed by airtime and
   repair, chosen default 8. Both are board settings in CONFIG link. The
   board applies them when it plans; repeaters never know which is which.
7. **The wrapper's fixed cost no longer grows with depth upward** (§5). UP
   is 5 bytes at any depth, so pictures (which go up) keep a **217-byte**
   payload however far away the camera is (arithmetic). Only DOWN carries
   the source route, one byte a hop; down payload is sized once, at the
   format's maximum, **204 bytes** (arithmetic). The format reaches 16
   radio hops with no further byte.
8. **One root per repeater, now as a measured-in-arithmetic decision**
   (§4): N co-located meshes share the data airtime they would share
   anyway; what they duplicate is beacons, keepalives and boxes. The
   control plane of four meshes on one channel is about 0.5% to 5% of the
   channel by arithmetic (derived lower bound, depending on rate), and
   separate meshes keep a freedom shared repeaters lose: boards on
   different routers can use different channels. Multi-root is costed and
   the format reserves room for it.
9. **The mesh reports its health every 10 minutes from what is already
   arriving** (§15): no poll, no new message. The one counter message the
   first revision sent every minute now goes every 10 minutes with
   cumulative counters, so health costs **less** airtime than revision 1. A
   `MESH` command in SATS's shape; one verdict word on the panels; a
   verdict is a cause and an action, never a wall of figures.
10. **Self-healing in three tiers**: the frame that finds a dead parent
    takes the next parent; the board answers a PATH_ERR by routing down
    through the alternate; a full recompute follows. Every state a node
    can be in has a timed end (§10).
11. **No session is pinned to a path.** A path carrying a caller is not
    moved for a merely better one (soft pinning, §12).
12. **The board's pairings must grow, and "one pairing rather than
    thirty" is wrong as worded.** Keys are end to end, so the board holds
    one per sat; what stops being the wall is the 20-entry ESP-NOW peer
    table. Pairings go to 32 with the mesh on: heap, not static DRAM (§7.1).

## 0. What a mesh buys, and what it costs on one channel

**A mesh here buys reach and survivability, never capacity.** Every
ESP-NOW node sits on the board's router channel (LINK.md, Channels): the
board is a station and must be on its AP's channel, and every peer follows
the board. Zigbee spreads hops across 16 channels; we have one. So:

- **Every hop is the same air again.** A frame that crosses D radio hops is
  transmitted D times on one channel (arithmetic), and a repeater cannot
  transmit while a neighbour in its range does.
- **All traffic converges on the board.** The last hop into the board
  carries the whole mesh's traffic. Repeaters at the edge add no capacity
  at the centre.
- **That air is also the board's own Wi-Fi.** The router channel carries
  the callers' telnet and SSH. LINK.md records the board's gateway pings
  rising from 1-4 ms to 7-21 ms when the link ran at 1 Mbps (measured, the
  1.2.0 bench). Mesh airtime is a Rule no. 1 question, and §19 gates it on
  the board's own gateway latency.
- **A few dozen boxes buy coverage, not callers.** Ten caller lines a
  board, whatever feeds them (the callin spec, §8).

## 1. Shape: a tree rooted at the board

### 1.1 Roles

| Role | Who | Forwards | Holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **root** | the board | **never** | every member's pairing, every member's last report, the plan, the health roll-up |
| **repeater** | a sat that says it can repeat (§1.2) | other members' frames, sealed, unopened | its own pairing with the board, its neighbour table, its parent set and children from the plan |
| **leaf** | any member that does not repeat | nothing | its own pairing, its neighbour table, its parent set |

A **member** is a sat paired with the board (an ordinary LINK.md pairing).
Repeaters are members too: a repeater needs its own session with the board
for its reports and its plan. "Holds no key" means no key for the traffic
it forwards, as sat-types §1 already said.

### 1.2 Repeating is a role, not a kind

The callin spec made "repeater" one of the gateway sat's run-time roles.
This spec generalises it: **any member may repeat if it says so**, and the
board decides whether to use it.

- **A sat that sleeps never repeats.** Zigbee's shape: mains-powered
  routers relay, end devices do not. A member reports `roles` in its sealed
  NEIGH_REPORT (§8): bit 0 can repeat, bit 1 stays awake, bit 2 on a
  permanent supply, **bit 3 carries callers** (it is a door sat or a
  gateway sat with a caller role on). Bit 3 is what puts a member under the
  interactive depth (§3).
- **Not in HELLO.** HELLO's tag is computed over exactly 39 bytes and sits
  at offset 39 (read: `kHelloLen`, link.cpp:265 and 1119-1123), so a field
  cannot be added to HELLO without breaking every paired device.

### 1.3 Why the board computes the routes

The settled rule is that the board does not route. What that rule protects
is the loop: no frame of anybody else's may cost the board per-frame work.
**Computing routes is not forwarding**:

- The board receives reports a few times a minute per node and recomputes
  at most once every 2 s (chosen, §7.4): a bounded job on the background
  runner, of the class the pairing arithmetic already is.
- **It is the only place the answer to §2 exists.** Only the board holds a
  key with every member.
- **It adds no single point of failure.** Every path ends at the board.
- **Repeaters still route** in the sense Rule no. 1 cares about: they pick
  the next hop for every frame and fail over on their own.

§19 item 9 measures the computation; if it ever shows in the loop the
answer is to move it, not to distribute it.

### 1.4 Size

- **32 members a mesh** (chosen, `kMeshMax`), ids 1 to 32, one byte on the
  wire (0 is the board, 255 is "none"). Bounded by the board's heap (§7.1)
  and by the plan computation, which is unmeasured; the format reaches 254.
- **12 children a repeater, and 12 for the board** (chosen). A node's
  ESP-NOW peer table has 20 entries (read: `ESP_NOW_MAX_TOTAL_PEER_NUM`).
  A repeater needs a radio peer for each node it sends unicast to: up to 3
  parents and its children. 3 + 12 + the broadcast address = 16, leaving 4
  for the direct pairings a shared sat holds with other boards.
- **Depth: §3.** Depth and member count are different limits and §3.5
  shows why they are easy to conflate.

## 2. Trust: how a route is authenticated

### 2.1 The problem

The payload is sealed end to end (AES-128-CCM, the 20-byte header as
associated data; LINK.md, Frames). **Routing metadata lives outside that
envelope by construction**: a repeater has to read where a frame is going.
In a self-healing mesh the network chooses the path, and anything in radio
range that can make itself look like a good next hop can pull traffic
through itself. It cannot read what it attracts. It can drop it, delay it,
and see who is talking and how much.

### 2.2 What existing protocols do

| Protocol | How routing is protected | What it does not stop |
|---|---|---|
| **Zigbee** | one 128-bit **network key** held by every device secures the network layer, routing included; link keys are pairwise and application-layer only | anybody who extracts the network key from one device (physical extraction is documented) is a full member and can forge routing for the whole network |
| **Babel** (RFC 8967) | an HMAC on every packet, under keys shared by the routers on a link | outsiders only; every holder of the key may announce any route. RFC 8968 (DTLS) still trusts any authenticated neighbour's routes |
| **RPL** (RFC 6550) | unsecured (lean on the link layer), preinstalled (a shared key on control messages), authenticated (preinstalled keys admit only leaves; routers fetch keys from an authority, details left to a later specification that, as far as the papers found, was never written) | deployments run unsecured mode over 802.15.4 link-layer security; preinstalled mode is open to insiders for want of per-sender authentication |
| **batman-adv** | none of its own: originator messages are unauthenticated, links underneath are secured instead (802.11s with SAE) | an authenticated node can still claim a perfect route |
| **Espressif `esp-now` component** | flooding with `forward_ttl` and an RSSI floor | not a trust mechanism |
| **RPL non-storing mode** (RFC 6550 §9, RFC 6554) | not security, the shape: nodes report parents to the root, the root holds the topology and puts the full route in each downward packet | |

**A group key keeps outsiders out and does nothing about a member that
lies**, and the one design with an answer for members (RPL's authenticated
mode) needed a key authority and never shipped.

### 2.3 What fits a cheap ESP32 paired with a four-digit code

- **A group key: rejected.** Reading a key out of an unencrypted ESP32's
  flash takes a USB cable; one extracted key forges routing for the whole
  mesh; revocation means re-keying every member through the mesh under
  attack; and it would be a long-term secret with no physical act behind
  it, where every key today comes from a pairing a sysop stood beside.
- **Signatures: rejected.** A P-256 signature is 64 bytes and a certificate
  about 140 (arithmetic) against a 250-byte frame, verified per beacon at a
  cost on the ESP32 nobody here has measured. Their only purpose would be
  letting nodes verify each other, which §2.4 removes.
- **The board as the only reader: adopted.** RPL non-storing mode with its
  control plane sealed end to end under keys that already exist.

### 2.4 The rules

- **T1. Routing information is believed only by the board, and only when
  sealed.** NEIGH_REPORT, PATH_ERR and MESH_STATS are sealed session-0
  messages under the member's `k_sess`. Anything else on the air about
  topology is a hint.
- **T2. A member believes only the board about its own place.** Rank,
  parents, children and hop cap arrive in a PLAN sealed under its own
  `k_sess`. No node derives its rank from a neighbour, so there is no rank
  to poison and no count to infinity (§9).
- **T3. A link exists only when both ends report it.** Cost uses the worse
  of the two RSSI figures and the worse of the two delivery figures (§7.3).
  The figures are taken by each receiving radio, so a spoofer can neither
  raise one nor write the other end's report.
- **T4. A path is proved by end-to-end acknowledgement, and by nothing
  else.** A MAC ACK proves a radio heard a frame (LINK.md already refuses
  to trust one for the channel), not that it reached the board.
- **T5. A beacon is a hint, and a hint that fails is remembered.** A
  neighbour that does not produce an authenticated board answer within 1 s
  (chosen) is not tried again for 60 s (chosen).
- **T6. Wrappers are not authenticated, and need not be.** Anything a
  repeater can do by rewriting one ends in a frame that does not open or
  never arrives: the power to drop, which a repeater has anyway. A forged
  origin id makes the board try the wrong key and drop the frame, as a
  forged MAC does today.
- **T7. Revocation is forgetting one pairing.** No group key, so nothing to
  rotate.
- **T8. Proxied pairing must be checked.** Through a mesh the pairing
  window spans the mesh's area and repeaters sit in the exchange, so a
  pairing that arrives proxied (§11) is not offered unchecked. The code is
  what rules out a repeater swapping public keys (sat-types §1).

### 2.5 What an attacker can do, and what limits it

| Attacker | Can | Cannot | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outsider in range** | spoof a member's MAC and drop what is sent to it; beacon our root at a low rank; send junk for proxying; jam | read, alter or replay any frame; write any report the board believes; make a link look better than both ends measure it | T3, T4, T5; proxy rate limits (§11); every drop counted by reason |
| **Captured member** | the above, plus false neighbour reports, and dropping or delaying what it carries; see which members are active, how much, when | read others' traffic; make another member report a link to it; attract traffic over a link that does not physically exist | T3; delivery accounting (§7.3); the health verdict "repeater losing frames" (§15); T7 |
| **Wormhole** (an outsider tunnels real frames, so two distant members genuinely hear each other) | create a link both ends really report, then drop what crosses it | anything a captured member cannot | delivery accounting. Packet leashes (Hu, Perrig, Johnson, 2003) need tight clocks or positions we do not have, so we price loss rather than prevent the link |
| **Anybody listening** | see keystroke timing: a CALLIN keystroke is one frame | read keystrokes | nothing new: today's direct link shows the same timing. Keystroke timing leaks information (Song, Wagner, Tian, 2001); the mesh adds places to listen from, not a new leak |

**What the design cannot stop**: a repeater on a path can always drop or
delay it. The mesh notices and routes around; the sysop revokes.

## 3. Depth: two limits, set by what a sat is for

### 3.1 What sets the ceiling, and what revision 1 got wrong

**Depth** here is the number of radio hops between the board and a
member: 1 for a sat the board hears directly, 2 behind one repeater, and so
on. A member's rank is its depth along its planned path.

Revision 1 gave one limit, 4 radio hops, for all traffic, and argued it
from latency. **The argument was latency, but what actually held the
ceiling there was the format**: its route-length and path-record fields
were two bits wide, so 4 was the most it could express, and its UP wrapper
recorded every repeater crossed, so every hop of allowed depth cost a byte
of payload in both directions, pictures included. A camera behind a fifth
repeater was refused by a two-bit field, not by anything a camera cares
about. That was the error: one limit for traffic with two different needs,
frozen into a format that made raising it cost every frame.

In revision 2:

- **Interactive depth is set by latency.** A keystroke goes up and its
  echo comes down; each hop adds to both (§13).
- **Data depth is set by airtime and repair, not latency.** A picture does
  not care about 200 ms. It does cost D transmissions of the board's own
  channel, it is more likely to need a retry across more hops, and a deeper
  member stays dark longer after a router changes channel (§10.5).
- **The format no longer sets either**: it reaches 16 hops (§3.3), and
  depth costs payload only on the DOWN direction, one byte a hop, sized
  once (§3.2).

### 3.2 What each hop costs in payload

All arithmetic from the frame format (§5). A meshed frame is
250 = 20 header + 8 tag + wrapper + payload. Direct (depth 1) frames carry
no wrapper and keep 222.

| Depth D (radio hops) | UP wrapper | UP payload | DOWN wrapper (2 + D) | DOWN payload (220 - D) | Revision 1's layout (219 - D, and only to D = 4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | none | 222 | none | 222 | 222 |
| 3 | 5 | **217** | 5 | **217** | 216 |
| 6 | 5 | **217** | 8 | **214** | could not express |
| 10 | 5 | **217** | 12 | **210** | could not express |
| 16 | 5 | **217** | 18 | **204** | could not express |

- **UP costs nothing per hop.** Revision 1 appended each repeater's id to
  an UP frame so the board could see the path taken. That record is
  replaced by a one-byte `detour` field (§5.2) naming the first node that
  used a parent other than its first, which is what the board actually
  needed from it (a local failover happened, and where). Pictures and
  sensor readings, which go up, keep 217 bytes at any depth.
- **DOWN costs one byte a hop**, because a source route must name every
  hop. Down traffic is snap requests, settings, acknowledgements and a
  caller's screen output.
- **Sizing, decided: up 217 and down 204 for every meshed member, whatever
  its current depth.** A message is cut into fragments when it is queued,
  and `len` and `nfrag` are inside the authenticated header, so no
  repeater can re-cut a fragment for a longer path; a member rerouted
  deeper mid-message must still fit. Sizing down at the format's maximum
  (16) means no setting change and no reroute can ever make a queued
  fragment too big. The price is 13 bytes of down payload against the
  depth-3 figure, about 6% more frames for down traffic (arithmetic).
  Revision 1's single 215 is withdrawn.

### 3.3 The deepest the format expresses, and the deepest that is sane

- **The format: 16 radio hops without another byte** (arithmetic). The
  DOWN route length and the "next" index are four bits each in one control
  byte (1 to 16 ids, a source route has no loop to need a TTL); the UP TTL
  is four bits (16 hops). Seventeen would need a wider control byte.
- **Sane on one channel: not something arithmetic settles, so it is
  chosen and then measured.** What grows with depth, all of it on the
  board's own channel:
  - **Airtime**: D transmissions per frame (arithmetic), so a picture at
    depth 8 occupies the channel eight times as long as a direct one.
  - **Path delivery**: if each hop delivers a fraction p of what it is
    given after its own retries, a path delivers p to the power D. For
    illustration only, with p assumed rather than measured: p = 0.99
    gives 0.92 at D = 8 and 0.85 at D = 16; p = 0.95 gives 0.66 and 0.44
    (derived). End-to-end retries recover the rest, each recovery costing
    another D transmissions.
  - **Round trip against the retry timer**: the engine's first end-to-end
    retry is at 150 ms (read: `kRtoMs`, link.h:304). With the estimated
    forwarding cost of 2-6 ms a hop (§13), a depth-16 round trip is about
    64-192 ms plus the board's tick (derived), so deep paths would retry
    before the answer could arrive and spend another D transmissions
    each time. A data depth past a handful needs the adaptive timer in
    §17 item 13.
  - **Repair after a router changes channel** re-forms the mesh one layer
    at a time (§10.5), so a deeper mesh is dark longer; the time per layer
    is unmeasured.
  - **A deep sleeping sat stays awake longer** waiting for its
    acknowledgement, which is battery.
- **Decided defaults** (chosen): **interactive 3, data 8**, format maximum
  16. Eight is a judgement, not a measurement: it keeps a picture's
  airtime at eight transmissions and the re-form after a router change at
  eight layers, half the format's reach. §19 items 1, 4 and 6 move both.

### 3.4 Configured, not constant, and what a sysop chooses

Both are settings, board side, in CONFIG link, live (a change triggers a
recompute). The rows, wording for tty-ux to finish:

| Key | Label (80) | Range | Default | What the sysop is choosing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mesh_call_depth` | `Hops a caller may be from the board` | 1 .. `mesh_data_depth` | 3 | **how far away a caller's terminal may be, against how laggy it feels.** Warns past 3: "Each hop adds to every keystroke's echo." |
| `mesh_data_depth` | `Hops a camera or sensor may be from the board` | 1 .. 16 | 8 | **how far away a camera or sensor may be, against how much of the board's own Wi-Fi channel the mesh uses and how long far sats stay dark after the router changes channel.** Warns past 8: "Deep paths use the callers' Wi-Fi channel more and recover more slowly." |

Two things the rows must make clear, because both are easy to misread:

- **A depth limit is a ceiling, not a target.** The board always uses the
  cheapest acceptable route. Raising a limit changes nothing for a sat
  that already has a short route; it only admits sats that are farther
  away. Lowering one cuts off only the sats beyond it.
- **The interactive limit governs sessions, not membership.** A member
  that carries callers but sits deeper than `mesh_call_depth` stays in the
  mesh (its pictures, its status and its own reports still flow) and
  refuses new door or CALLIN sessions with a reason the sysop sees in MESH
  (§15) and a gateway shows its caller. A member already carrying a
  session that is forced deeper by a failure keeps the session (laggy
  beats dropped) and is flagged.

How the board applies them, with repeaters none the wiser: the plan gives
every member a rank no deeper than the data depth, prefers paths within
the interactive depth for members with roles bit 3, and puts each
member's own cap in its PLAN (`hopCap`), which becomes the TTL on its UP
frames. A repeater only decrements a TTL and follows a source route.

### 3.5 Depth is not node count

The two are separate limits with separate causes, and a 20-member mesh
makes the difference concrete:

- **Node count is bounded by fan-out**: 12 children a node (§1.4). Depth 1
  holds at most 12 members; depth 2 holds up to 12 + 12 x 12 = 156
  (arithmetic), so from depth 2 on the 32-member cap binds, not depth.
- **Depth is bounded by geography**: how many radio hops separate the
  board from its farthest sat. More members usually make a mesh wider,
  not deeper. A mesh gets deep only because something is far away or
  behind a wall.

What 20 members look like at each depth:

| Depth in use | What the 20 are | Typical place |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | impossible: the board takes 12 children, so 8 must sit behind a repeater | |
| 2 | e.g. 4 repeaters within the board's reach, 16 sats spread among them | a house and its garden |
| 3 | the same, plus one repeater out at the edge with a few sats behind it | the barn beyond the garden |
| 6 | at least 5 of the 20 are repeaters in a chain; the rest hang off it | a fairground midway, a long drive |
| 10 | at least 9 are repeaters in a line; 11 are left for everything else | a road or a fence line |
| 16 | at least 15 of the 20 are one line of repeaters; 5 are left, and every frame from the far end is sent 16 times | possible, and the reason the data default is 8 |

So the question "how big can the mesh get" has two answers: 32 members,
at whatever depth geography needs, and a depth that is about distance.

## 4. Several boards on one channel

### 4.1 The case

A fairground with four boards on one router is four meshes on one channel.
Revision 1 kept one root per repeater and deferred the rest. This section
costs both alternatives so the limit is a decision.

### 4.2 What N co-located meshes cost in airtime

**The data plane is the same either way.** A picture from a sat to its
board crosses D hops whether the repeaters on its path serve one board or
four. Sharing repeaters does not reduce what is sent; it can only shorten
a path when a repeater placed for one board happens to sit well for
another.

**The control plane is what N meshes duplicate**: each mesh's repeaters
beacon, and each member pings its own board through its own path. Using
ESP-NOW's on-air overhead of 43 bytes a frame (read: Espressif's frame
format, 24 MAC header + 1 category + 3 OUI + 4 random + 7 element header +
4 FCS) and nominal 802.11 timings (192 us preamble at 1 Mbps; 20 us
preamble and 96 bits a 4 us symbol at 24 Mbps), ignoring backoff,
inter-frame spaces and acknowledgements, so every figure below is a
**lower bound, derived**:

| Frame | Bytes on air | At 24 Mbps | At 1 Mbps |
|---|---|---|---|
| MESH_BEACON (20 header + 10) | 73 | about 0.05 ms | about 0.78 ms (broadcasts go at ESP-NOW's 1 Mbps default) |
| PING, sealed, UP-wrapped (20 + 9 + 8 + 5) | 85 a hop | about 0.05 ms a hop | about 0.87 ms a hop |
| NEIGH_REPORT, sealed, UP-wrapped, 20 neighbours | 241 a hop | about 0.10 ms a hop | about 2.1 ms a hop |
| a full 250-byte data frame | 293 a hop | about 0.12 ms a hop | about 2.5 ms a hop |

One mesh of 20 members with 8 repeaters at an average depth of 3, beacons
every 10 s, PINGs every 5 s, reports at the 60 s floor: about **1.3 ms of
air a second (0.13%)** with every hop at 24 Mbps, about **13 ms a second
(1.3%)** with every hop at 1 Mbps (derived, lower bounds). **Four such
meshes: about 0.5% to 5% of the channel**, before any picture or
keystroke. The direct link already pays the PINGs at depth 1; the mesh
multiplies them by depth and adds beacons.

What sharing repeaters would save of that: the beacons (one beacon could
carry several roots) and the extra repeaters' own PINGs. The members'
PINGs remain, one set per board. So **multi-root saves boxes, not
airtime**: four boards wanting coverage of one field need four sets of
repeaters with one root, one set with shared ones.

### 4.3 What a repeater serving several boards would take

Costed, not built:

- **Keys: no change to the model.** A repeater holds no key for anything
  it forwards, whatever board it is going to. It would hold its own
  pairing with each root it serves, which the peer role already supports
  (up to 5 boards, read: `kHosts`, link.h:295).
- **Ids and wrappers**: member ids are per board, so a forwarded frame
  must name its root. One byte, a root tag each board picks at random and
  a shared repeater reports clashes on (sealed, so the second board
  re-rolls). The format reserves **0x85 and 0x86** for rooted UP and DOWN
  (§5.1); version 1 drops them.
- **Beacons**: one beacon carrying a (root tag, rank) pair per root, 2
  bytes a root more than today. No more frames.
- **Neighbour table**: a rank and an id per root per neighbour, about 6
  bytes more an entry at 4 roots (arithmetic), 120 bytes for 20 entries.
- **Plans**: a parent set and a children list per root.
- **The hard part: independent planners sharing one radio's peer
  table.** A repeater has 19 unicast peers to give (§1.4) and four boards
  that never talk to each other would each plan children onto it. The
  repeater would report its free slots to every root and refuse a child
  past them (a PATH_ERR "full"), and boards would converge through
  refusals. That is a resource two planners contend for with no
  coordinator, which is exactly the shape that oscillates, and it is
  where the work would be.
- **One channel forced**: a repeater with one radio can serve several
  boards only if they are all on one channel. Separate meshes can sit on
  separate channels when the boards are on separate routers, and then
  share no air at all.

### 4.4 Decided

**One root per repeater in version 1.** Co-located meshes cost a control
plane that is a small fraction of the channel by arithmetic, and the data
plane is the same either way; what one root really costs is hardware, a
box per board where boxes overlap. Against that, multi-root needs
cross-board arbitration of a shared peer table and forces every board in
a shared area onto one channel. The rooted wrapper values are reserved so
a later version adds it without breaking repeaters already planted. §19
item 12 measures two co-located meshes against one before the arithmetic
here is trusted.

A sat shared by several boards (LINK.md, "One satellite, several boards")
keeps its direct pairings with all of them, but routes through only one
board's mesh.

## 5. The wrapper

### 5.1 Telling a wrapped frame from a plain one

A plain frame's first byte is `ver` = 1 (LINK.md, Frames). A wrapped
frame's first byte has bit 7 set. A board or sat built before the mesh
reads 0x8x as an unknown version and drops it, counted.

| First byte | Wrapper | Sent by | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x01 | none | anybody, one radio hop | a plain frame, as today |
| 0x81 | **UP** | a member with an id, and each repeater on the way | a frame for the board |
| 0x82 | **DOWN** | the board, and each repeater on the way | a frame for one member, source-routed |
| 0x83 | **PROXY_UP** | the first repeater that heard it | a clear frame from a device the mesh has no id for |
| 0x84 | **PROXY_DOWN** | the board, and each repeater on the way | the board's answer to such a device |
| 0x85, 0x86 | **reserved**: rooted UP and DOWN (§4.3) | | dropped in version 1 |

Every other value with bit 7 set is reserved and dropped. All fields are
single bytes or MACs, so there is no endianness to get wrong.

### 5.2 Layouts

**UP (0x81), 5 bytes at any depth**

```
 off  field
   0  0x81
   1  ctl     bits 0-3: t, hops this frame may still take less one (0-15);
              bits 4-7 zero
   2  origin  the member's id, from its PLAN
   3  srank   the rank of the node that sent THIS hop (rewritten each hop)
   4  detour  the id of the first node on the way that sent it to a parent
              other than its first; 0xFF none
```

The origin sets `t` to its PLAN's `hopCap` less one. A repeater receiving
`t` = 0 drops the frame (it would need another hop), otherwise sends it on
with `t` less one. A node that sends to a parent other than its first
writes its own id into `detour` if it still says 0xFF.

**DOWN (0x82), 2 + D bytes**

```
 off  field
   0  0x82
   1  ctl     bits 0-3: n - 1, the route's length (1-16); bits 4-7: next,
              the index of the id that should be receiving this hop
   2  route   n ids: the repeaters in order, the destination member last
```

A repeater checks `route[next]` is its own id, adds one to `next`, and
sends to the MAC of `route[next]` from its children list. A source route
cannot loop, so DOWN needs no TTL. A member the board hears directly is
sent plain frames.

**PROXY_UP (0x83), 10 bytes**

```
 off  field
   0  0x83
   1  ctl     bits 0-3: t, as UP
   2  mac     the device's MAC, 6 bytes, as the first repeater received it
   8  rssi    i8, the device as the first repeater heard it
   9  first   the first repeater's id
```

The board answers along its own route to `first`, which it knows from the
plan, so the proxy needs no path record.

**PROXY_DOWN (0x84), 8 + n bytes**

```
 off  field
   0  0x84
   1  ctl     bits 0-3: n - 1 repeaters (1-15 used); bits 4-7: next
   2  route   n repeater ids, the first repeater that heard the device last
 2+n  mac     the device's MAC, 6 bytes: the last repeater sends to it
```

**A repeater never touches the frame after the wrapper.**

### 5.3 Sizes

| | Bytes | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| ESP-NOW frame | 250 | read: `ESP_NOW_MAX_DATA_LEN`, link.h:70 |
| header + tag | 28 | read: link.h:71-72 |
| direct sealed payload | 222 | read: `kPayloadMax`, link.h:73 |
| meshed UP payload, any depth | 217 | arithmetic |
| meshed DOWN payload, sized at depth 16 | 204 | arithmetic |
| largest proxied clear frame: PAIR_HELLO 115 + PROXY_DOWN at 15 repeaters 23 | 138 | arithmetic, fits |

The callin spec's per-path table (222, 220, 219) and revision 1's 215 are
superseded.

## 6. Neighbours and beacons

### 6.1 MESH_BEACON (family 0, type 26, clear)

Sent by a node with roles bits 0 and 1 set and an id, broadcast every
10 s (chosen) with up to 2 s of random jitter, and at once when its rank
changes (at most once a second). Also sent **unicast, in answer to a
DISCOVER**, at most once a second to any one MAC, so a node sweeping
channels finds a repeater within LINK.md's 20 ms dwell (read: `kDwellMs`,
link.h:310).

```
 off size field
   0   6  root     the board's MAC: which mesh this is
   6   1  rank     its depth in its current plan; 0xFF "cut off"
   7   1  chan     the channel it believes the board is on
   8   1  free     children it can still take (12 less its count)
   9   1  roles
```

- **A rank is advertised only while it is fresh.** A node that has not
  received a sealed frame from the board for 15 s (chosen) beacons 0xFF,
  so a cut-off subtree does not attract orphans into itself (§10.5).
- A beacon is believed by nobody about anything (T5). Its sender's MAC
  and the RSSI the receiver measured are the useful part.
- `clearType` (link.h:116) gains this one type. It grants nothing.

### 6.2 The neighbour table, on every member

One entry for each MAC heard sending a MESH_BEACON for this root, or any
mesh frame, in the last 35 s (chosen: three and a half beacons).

| Field | Bytes |
|---|---|
| mac | 6 |
| id (255 unknown) | 1 |
| rank, as last beaconed: a hint | 1 |
| rssi, i8, exponential average, 1/4 new | 1 |
| heardAt (`plat::since` against it, §18) | 4 |
| sentOk, sentFail: MAC acknowledgements of unicasts to it, halved when either passes 1,000 | 2 + 2 |
| failRun: send callbacks in a row that said FAIL | 1 |
| rate: 24 Mbps or the 1 Mbps fallback, **its own** (§18) | 1 |
| triedAt (T5), 0 for never | 4 |
| flags: parent, child, suspect | 1 |
| **total** | **24** (arithmetic): **480 bytes** for 20 entries, heap, allocated when the mesh starts |

When full, the entry heard longest ago that is not a parent or a child
goes; a beacon flood cannot displace a working path.

### 6.3 What a node reports

NEIGH_REPORT (§8) carries, for up to 20 neighbours: MAC, average RSSI and
a delivery byte: bits 0-6 `sentOk * 126 / (sentOk + sentFail)`, 127 when
nothing has been sent to it; **bit 7 set when the link is on the 1 Mbps
fallback**, which the health roll-up needs for its airtime figure and
costs no byte (§15). Sent when a neighbour appears or goes, when an RSSI
moves 6 dB or more from what was last reported, or when a parent fails; at
most once every 5 s, and every 60 s regardless (all chosen).

## 7. The board: pairings, reports and the plan

### 7.1 Pairings, and the fan-out correction

With keys end to end the board needs a key per sat. What the mesh changes:

- **Radio peers.** Today the engine registers every pairing as an ESP-NOW
  peer (`Io::addPeer`, link.h:177), and the table holds 20. In the mesh
  the board's radio peers are only its children (12 at most); `addPeer`
  for a far member is a no-op in the mesh layer. So the ESP-NOW table
  stops being the wall for the number of sats.
- **Pairings grow to 32 with the mesh on.** `struct Engine::Peer` is
  **240 bytes** (read: g++ 13 on the host build, 2026-10-09; the ESP32
  figure is to be read off the target ELF). 32 pairings is 7,680 bytes
  against today's 8 at 1,920: **+5,760 bytes of heap and no static DRAM**
  (arithmetic), from PSRAM where the board has it.
- **The pairings file gains an id column** (version 3, `#link-peers 3`),
  so a restart does not renumber the mesh.

### 7.2 What the board keeps per member

On the heap, beside the pairings (arithmetic from the field list):

| | Bytes |
|---|---|
| last report: up to 12 member neighbours (id, rssi, delivery byte) | 36 |
| roles, rank, plan version sent, last report time, flags | 8 |
| parent set (3 ids), children count | 4 |
| delivery accounting over routes through it | 8 |
| health (§15.4) | 36 |
| **per member** | **92**, so **2,944** for 32 |

**Board mesh heap in all**: about 2.9 KB plus 5.8 KB of extra pairings,
**about 8.7 KB** (an estimate from struct sizes, to be read off the
target). The link already takes about 19 KB while on (LINK.md, Budgets).

### 7.3 Link cost

For a link A to X, used only when both A's and X's latest reports list
each other (T3):

- `rssi = min(A's figure for X, X's figure for A)`
- `deliv = min(A's delivery to X, X's delivery to A)`, ignoring unknowns.
- `cost = 1 + rssiPenalty(rssi) + lossPenalty(deliv) + ratePenalty`,
  where 1 is the hop itself and the rate penalty applies when either end
  reports the link on the 1 Mbps fallback.

The penalty functions get no numbers here: they are fitted to §19 item 4's
RSSI-against-loss measurements. Their shape is decided: flat above the
RSSI where the bench sees no loss, steep below it, and a delivery under a
floor priced as nearly unusable.

**Delivery accounting.** The board knows the route of every frame it
sends down and the origin and `detour` of every frame it receives. A frame
sent down and never acknowledged end to end counts against that route. A
repeater on two or more lossy routes while routes avoiding it are clean
gets a cost penalty on every link through it. Attribution across a route is
a heuristic and is described as one.

### 7.4 The plan

- **Computed on the runner**, never on the loop. Output, per member: a
  rank and an ordered parent set of up to 3, each of a **strictly lower
  rank** (§9), and its `hopCap`; per repeater, its children with MACs.
- **Algorithm**: a shortest-path tree from the board over confirmed links,
  repeat-capable members as interior nodes only, no node given more than
  12 children, no member placed deeper than `mesh_data_depth`, and members
  with roles bit 3 preferring paths within `mesh_call_depth` (§3.4).
  Second and third parents are the next-cheapest neighbours of lower rank.
  At 32 members and 12 links each this is a few hundred edge relaxations
  (arithmetic); its time on an ESP32 is unmeasured (§19 item 9).
- **When**: at most once every 2 s (chosen), and only when an input
  changed: a member up or down, a report moving a link's cost by a
  quarter or more, a PATH_ERR, a pairing added or forgotten, a depth
  setting changed.
- **Hysteresis**: a parent set changes only when a parent failed or a new
  path is at least 25% cheaper (chosen).
- **Plan version**: 16 bits, compared by wrap-safe difference.
- **Distribution**: a PLAN (§8) to each member whose plan changed, sealed,
  reliable, session 0.

## 9. Loop control

1. **Ranks strictly decrease toward the board, by construction.** Every
   parent the board gives a node has a lower rank, so upward forwarding
   along any parent approaches the board. Downward routes are source
   routes and cannot loop.
2. **Data-path rank check** (RPL's data-path validation, RFC 6550 §11.2):
   a repeater receiving an UP frame whose `srank` is not greater than its
   own rank drops it, counts it, and sends PATH_ERR reason 4; the board
   re-sends both plans. This catches the one transient a plan change can
   make.
3. **TTL on UP**, at most 16 hops (§5.2): the last defence, bounding the
   airtime any fault can spend.

**No count to infinity**: no node learns its rank from anybody but the
board (T2), and a cut-off node advertises 0xFF (§6.1). **Nothing floods**:
no frame type is ever rebroadcast, so there is no duplicate cache to get
wrong.

## 10. Failure, detection and repair

### 10.1 Upward: the frame that finds the failure takes the new path

A node sending UP to parent p:

1. On FAIL it resends to p once at once, and once more at 1 Mbps (the
   existing per-neighbour fallback, LINK.md).
2. If both fail, **the same frame goes to the next parent**, `detour` is
   written (§5.2), and p is marked suspect.
3. Three frames in a row that needed step 2 for p (chosen) make p failed
   until a new PLAN, and the node sends an urgent NEIGH_REPORT through
   the parent that worked.

Detection is driven by traffic: a node carrying a session finds a dead
parent at the next keystroke; an idle node at its next PING (read:
`kPingMs` 5 s, link.h:307), and loses nothing by finding out late.

T4 covers a parent that acknowledges and drops: a node whose end-to-end
retries reach their third try while the parent's MAC acknowledgements say
fine marks that parent failed as in step 3.

### 10.2 Downward: the board takes the alternate at once

A repeater that cannot reach the next id drops the frame and sends
PATH_ERR. The board routes that member's next frames through its **second
parent** at once, a table lookup; the lost frame is resent by the engine's
ordinary end-to-end retry and takes the new route. A recompute follows on
its schedule.

### 10.3 The node's states, every one with an end

All durations chosen.

| State | Means | Leaves when | And at most |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PLANNED** | holds a fresh PLAN | all parents failed: ORPHAN. No sealed board frame for 15 s: CUT_OFF | as long as it works |
| **CUT_OFF** | parents may be up, the board is not answering through them | a sealed board frame arrives: PLANNED | 15 s, then ORPHAN |
| **ORPHAN** | no usable parent | a sealed board frame arrives through a neighbour it tried: PLANNED | 30 s, then SCAN |
| **SCAN** | LINK.md's channel sweep | an authenticated board answer on a channel: PLANNED | one sweep (about a quarter of a second, LINK.md), then ORPHAN on the last good channel |
| **JOINING** | HELLO sent through a proxy | HELLO_ACK and PLAN: PLANNED | 5 s a try, then ORPHAN |

In ORPHAN a node tries the neighbours it hears, best beaconed rank first,
one at a time, with an urgent NEIGH_REPORT sent as if to a parent. A
repeater forwards UP from a member that is not its child at most 2 a
second for any one origin. A neighbour with no sealed board answer within
1 s is ruled out for 60 s (T5).

### 10.4 A repeater going away

Its children move to their next parents (§10.1); a child with none goes
ORPHAN. The board learns from the PATH_ERRs, the children's urgent
reports, and finally the repeater's missed PINGs (read: `kHostQuietMs`
20 s, link.h:309), which is the backstop, not the mechanism.

### 10.5 The router changing channel

The board moves; every member is left on the old channel. With the
freshness rule (§6.1) the old-channel mesh beacons 0xFF within 15 s, every
member reaches SCAN, and the mesh re-forms **one layer at a time**: the
board's children find it by LINK.md's sweep and beacon a fresh rank, the
next layer finds them. **A data depth of 8 means up to 8 layers.** The
total time is unmeasured and is §19 item 6.

## 11. Joining and pairing through the mesh

### 11.1 Proxying, instead of flooding

A device with no id sends its clear frames as it does today. A repeater
that hears one and may proxy it wraps it in PROXY_UP and sends it up its
own route. The board answers in PROXY_DOWN along its route to `first`.

- **What is proxied**: HELLO always; PAIR_HELLO, PAIR_NONCE and the sealed
  PAIR_ACK only while a PAIR_RELAY window is open. Nothing else.
- **Rate limits** (chosen): one proxied frame a second for any one device
  MAC, four a second in all. The board checks a HELLO's tag (`helloTag`,
  link.cpp:1122) and drops one that fails.
- **Several repeaters hear one device**: the board answers through the one
  whose PROXY_UP reported the strongest `rssi` among those arriving within
  50 ms (chosen) of the first.
- **Channel trust**: **a device settles on a channel only when an
  authenticated board frame arrives on it** (a HELLO_ACK, or a sealed
  frame). A MESH_BEACON is the hint that sends it looking.

### 11.2 Pairing a device the board cannot hear

LINK.md's exchange, unchanged byte for byte; only the transport differs.
The 4-digit code makes it safe with repeaters in the middle; hence T8.

### 11.3 Getting an id

Assigned by the board at pairing (lowest free, 1 to 32), kept in the
pairings file, learnt from the first PLAN after each HELLO.

## 12. A session across a reroute

- **Sessions are end to end and know nothing of paths** (`Sess::peer`,
  link.cpp:313); a reroute changes no session state.
- **Frames lost on the dead path are resent end to end** and take the new
  one; `seq` and the 64-wide replay window absorb duplicates and
  reordering (read: LINK.md).
- **No hard pinning.** A pinned path dies with the path. **Soft pinning**:
  while a member carries a family 2 or 3 session, the 25%-cheaper rule is
  not applied to it or to any repeater on its path.
- **What the caller sees.** When a CALLIN path breaks the board cannot
  reach the caller, so **the gateway sat says it**: after 2 s (chosen) of
  an unanswered upstream it shows a line in the board's voice that it is
  finding another way (wording for satwords and Rob). A door caller is held
  by the board, and LINK.md's "--> Lost the signal." applies when retries
  run out.
- **When retries run out**: 8 tries, 150 ms doubling to 1.2 s (read:
  link.h:303-305), about 7 s in all (arithmetic). §19 item 5 measures which
  side of 7 s a real repair falls.

## 13. Latency

**Everything in this section is derived except the bar it is measured
against.** The bar is today's keystroke echo with five callers on the
bench: **p50 4 ms, p95 12 ms** (measured, telnet over the LAN, CLAUDE.md).

A routed CALLIN keystroke goes up D radio hops, waits for the board's link
tick, and its echo comes down D hops. Two inputs:

- **f, the cost of one hop: an estimate, not a measurement.** CLAUDE.md
  and the callin spec carry "2-6 ms a hop forwarding from the receive
  callback"; sat-types §1, where it starts, calls it "a few milliseconds a
  hop, estimate, to measure".
- **The board's link tick: 20 ms** (read: LINK.md, Where the work runs). A
  keystroke arriving just after a tick waits up to 20 ms even on today's
  direct link.

Added echo, ms, as `2 x D x f` plus the worst tick (**derived**, every
cell):

| Radio hops D | f = 2 | f = 4 | f = 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (direct) | 24 | 28 | 32 |
| 2 | 28 | 36 | 44 |
| 3 | 32 | 44 | 56 |
| 4 | 36 | 52 | 68 |
| 8 | 52 | 84 | 116 |

These sit on top of the gateway's own hop to the caller, which the callin
spec found may be the biggest term (a phone in power save holding downlink
about 205 ms; derived there from the IDF's SoftAP defaults).

**Decisions:**

- **The mesh's share of a CALLIN echo is held to p95 50 ms. That figure is
  chosen in this document, not measured anywhere**: about four times
  today's p95 (arithmetic), with no perceptual study behind it. Rob may
  move it. It is the yardstick for `mesh_call_depth`'s default and warning,
  and must never be printed as a property of the board.
- **`mesh_call_depth` defaults to 3** (chosen): at the high estimate of f
  three hops is just over the chosen line, at the low one four is inside
  it. §19 items 1 and 3 replace the estimate and set the default.
- **Data traffic has no latency budget**; `mesh_data_depth` is governed by
  §3.3.
- **Every hop forwards from a task woken by the receive callback, never
  from a tick** (§16).
- **The board's 20 ms tick is the term to attack first**, outside this
  spec (§17 item 10).

## 14. Throughput and airtime

- **Bulk end to end is bounded by the window over the round trip**: 16
  fragments (64 with PSRAM) in flight (read: LINK.md), so throughput falls
  with depth before airtime does.
- **Every hop is the same air** (§0). sat-types §1 estimated one hop at
  30-45 KB/s against 54-88 KB/s direct (an estimate there; the direct
  figures are measured, LINK.md). For more hops no figure is given: it
  depends on which repeaters hear each other. §19 item 4 measures it.
- **The board's callers pay for the mesh's airtime.** Gated by §19 item 2,
  the test that kept LR mode off by default.
- **A 1 Mbps hop paces its whole path**, so a link's cost includes its
  rate (§7.3).
- The control plane's share is in §4.2: about 0.13% to 1.3% of the channel
  for one 20-member mesh (derived lower bound).

## 15. Health: what the mesh says about itself

### 15.1 The binding constraint: no extra airtime

Health is a **roll-up on the board of what already arrives**. It adds no
message, no field sent more often, and no poll:

| Input | Already sent for | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| NEIGH_REPORT | routing (§6.3) | on change, at most every 5 s, at least every 60 s |
| MESH_STATS | "where a frame died" | **every 10 minutes**, cumulative counters (§8) |
| PATH_ERR | repair (§10.2) | on failure |
| PING | LINK.md's heartbeat | every 5 s |
| UP wrapper's `origin` and `detour` | forwarding (§5.2) | every frame, read by the board's mesh layer as it strips the wrapper |
| the engine's own counts: frames, retries, drops, last heard, round trips | LINK.md | on the board, no air at all |
| the plan | routing (§7.4) | on the board |

Revision 1 sent MESH_STATS every minute; revision 2 sends it every 10
with cumulative counters, so adding health **reduces** airtime against the
first revision. The only explicit fetch is `MESH FRESH` (§15.5), a sysop
typing a command, which asks each member for one MESH_STATS at once: an
act, never a schedule.

### 15.2 Per member, for the period

- **parent**: planned (from the plan) and in use (the latest `detour`)
- **depth**: rank
- **link to its parent, each way**: RSSI and delivery as each end reports
  it, and whether either end is on 1 Mbps
- **frames forwarded** for others, and **dropped, by reason** (MESH_STATS
  differences, PATH_ERR counts)
- **time since last heard** (the engine's last authenticated frame)
- **reroutes**: parent changes sent in PLANs, plus detours seen

### 15.3 Per mesh, for the period

- **members reachable against members known**: heard within the last
  `kHostQuietMs` (read: 20 s) against pairings in the mesh
- **reroutes** in the period
- **deepest path in use**, and how many interactive members sit past
  `mesh_call_depth`
- **worst link in use**: the highest-cost link on any member's planned
  path
- **airtime estimate**: hop-transmissions counted exactly (every frame the
  board sent or received through the mesh, times its depth; the beacons
  and pings members are known to send), converted with §4.2's per-frame
  times at the rate each hop reports. **Always labelled a lower bound**:
  MAC-layer retries, backoff and other networks on the channel are
  invisible to the board.

### 15.4 Cost, and where it runs

- **Static DRAM: 0 bytes by design** (target, to be confirmed off the ELF
  of every env including the ESP32-CAM, 2,224 free at 1.2.2, read
  CLAUDE.md). The roll-up lives in the mesh's heap block, allocated when
  the mesh starts, nothing while it is off.
- **Heap**: 36 bytes a member (arithmetic from the fields: forwarded and
  drops by reason this period and last report's cumulative values to
  difference against, reroutes, detours, worst link cost, parent in use,
  the neighbour with most drops), 1,152 for 32; two mesh summaries, the
  current period and the last, about 48 bytes. Counted in §7.2.
- **Where**: counters are bumped where the board already handles the
  frame (the receive callback's wrapper strip, with atomics; the engine's
  existing counts). **The roll-up is a runner job** every 10 minutes
  (chosen), never on the loop. MESH reads the last finished roll-up and
  the live counters; it computes nothing heavy.
- **The console** logs one line when the verdict changes, never every
  period.

### 15.5 How a sysop sees it

**`MESH [n]`**, staff (`NODES`, as LINK's radio detail is), in SATS's
shape. Callers never see it: it is the radio and the topology, which SATS
keeps from callers. Layout for tty-ux to finish:

At 80 columns (the figures are illustrative, not from any mesh):

```
Mesh  unhealthy   18 of 20 up   depth 5   3 reroutes   air 0.4%+   as of 14:02
!  garden-cam not heard since 13:48, last via mast-2: check its power
!  mast-2 lost 41 of 312 frames it carried: move it closer to mast-1
 #  Name        Dep  Via        Link to parent    Fwd    Drop  Heard
 3  mast-1        1  board      -58/-61   ok      1,204     2   0:04
 7  mast-2        2  mast-1     -79/-83   1M        312    41   0:02
```

At 40 columns: the header and verdict lines wrap at a word; rows are
`#  Name      D Via     Drop Heard`.

- The header's first word is the verdict (§15.6). Each `!` line is one
  verdict with its cause and its action, worst first, at most three; then
  the rows, worst first.
- `MESH n` is one member in full: planned and actual parent, depth and
  hop cap, both directions of its link, forwarded, drops by reason,
  heard, reroutes this period, children, and whether it carries callers.
- `MESH FRESH` (sysop): one MESH_STATS from every member now (§15.1).
- SYS gains one line beside the existing Link line:
  `Mesh      healthy, 20 of 20 up, depth 3`.

**Panel boards** (every `BBS_HAS_LCD` board): the verdict word and
`up/known`, e.g. `Mesh 18/20`, in the colour the panel already uses for
its warning and risk states; nothing when the mesh is off. Placement and
glyph go through tty-ux first, as every panel change does.

### 15.6 What "unhealthy" means

A figure nobody can act on is noise, so **a verdict is a cause, a member
and an action**, and the header word is the worst verdict standing. Every
threshold is either pure logic or tied to the §19 measurement that sets
it; none is invented here.

| Verdict | Fires when | Says | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **member missing** | a known member was not heard in the whole period | its name, when and through whom it was last heard: "check its power" | unhealthy |
| **repeater losing frames** | a repeater dropped for reason 1 (send failed after resends) on every child it carried, or delivery accounting penalises it, while routes avoiding it are clean | its name, frames lost of carried: "move it, or check it" | unhealthy |
| **busy air** | the airtime lower bound passes the share at which §19 item 2 saw the callers' latency move | "the mesh is using the callers' Wi-Fi channel: lower the data depth or move repeaters" | unhealthy |
| **too deep for callers** | an interactive member sits past `mesh_call_depth` | its name and depth: "callers there are refused; add a repeater or raise the limit" | degraded |
| **flapping link** | the same member rerouted twice or more in the period (with 25% hysteresis, that is two failures, not two fluctuations) | the link: "something comes and goes between A and B" | degraded |
| **weak link** | a link in use whose delivery is under the floor §7.3's loss penalty treats as nearly unusable (set by §19 item 4), or that sat on 1 Mbps for most of the period | the two ends: "add a repeater between them" | degraded |
| (none) | | | **healthy** |

Healthy is the absence of every verdict, not a score. There is no number
for a sysop to interpret: the numbers are in the rows for whoever wants
them, and the verdicts are what to do.

## 16. Where the work runs

**On a repeater:**

| Where | What | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi task, receive callback | length check; by first byte, a frame to forward into the forwarding ring, anything for this node into its own rings | one copy of up to 250 bytes |
| **forwarding task**, woken by a notification from the callback, priority just under the Wi-Fi task | wrapper checks (`t`, rank, route index, rate limits), rewrite, next hop, `esp_now_send` | per frame; a ring of 16 frames (about 4.2 KB, arithmetic); full drops and counts |
| Wi-Fi task, send callback | the neighbour's ok and fail counts; for a forwarded frame that failed, a flag the task acts on (§10.1) | atomics |

A task rather than the callback because LINK.md's reading of the IDF is
"do not do lengthy operations in the callback function", and whether
`esp_now_send` may be called from the receive callback is not stated in the
5.3.1 documentation checked. §19 item 1 measures this shape.

**On the board:**

| Where | What | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi task, receive callback | strip the wrapper, map origin to the member's MAC, bump the health counters (atomics), hand the engine an ordinary frame | one copy |
| BBS loop, link tick | unchanged | LINK.md's figures |
| runner | bulk as today; the plan; the pairing arithmetic; the health roll-up | one job at a time |
| link tick, send side | wrap frames for far members in DOWN from the member's route: a lookup and up to 18 bytes | per frame |

**The board never forwards.** A frame arriving at the board is for the
board or it is dropped.

## 17. What the existing design has to change

The engine (`src/core/link.*`, which every sat builds) changes in items 1,
2 and 13 only.

1. **`Io` gains `payloadMax(direction)`**, default 222, answered 217 up and
   204 down by the mesh layer (§3.2); fragment sizing reads it.
2. **`Io::addPeer` stops meaning "every pairing is a radio peer"**: the
   mesh layer registers only the board's children with ESP-NOW.
3. **`kPeers` 8 becomes 32 with the mesh on** (link.h:294), heap only.
4. **The pairings file goes to version 3**, with an id column.
5. **Family 0 gains six types** (§8), `clearType` one.
6. **The channel-trust rule is generalised** (§11.1).
7. **A peer's rescan moves down a level**: parents and ORPHAN first, the
   sweep last (§10.3), including the sleeping sender's fast rescan.
8. **Unchecked pairing is refused when proxied** (T8).
9. **The callin spec's §7 and repeater page are superseded**: `rep_up`
   goes; `rep_hops` becomes the board's `mesh_call_depth` and
   `mesh_data_depth` (§3.4). sat-types §1's two-byte wrapper is
   superseded by the global id.
10. **Found, not this spec's to fix: the board's 20 ms link tick is the
    largest known term in a CALLIN keystroke's echo**, mesh or no mesh.
    Waking the loop when the control ring takes a frame would remove up to
    20 ms from every keystroke. It touches the loop's `select()`; a design
    question for the CALLIN lane.
11. **Found, not fixed: "one pairing rather than thirty"** in CLAUDE.md and
    revision 1's brief should read "one radio peer per neighbour rather
    than one per sat" (§7.1).
12. **The host radio needs a reach table** before any mesh code: the host's
    UDP medium lets everybody hear everybody, so no host test can prove a
    frame crossed a repeater.
13. **An adaptive end-to-end retry timer.** The fixed 150 ms first retry
    (read: `kRtoMs`) is shorter than a deep path's round trip at the
    estimated hop cost (§3.3, derived), and each premature retry spends D
    transmissions. The engine already stamps `sentAt` on every message;
    a smoothed round trip and variance per peer, RFC 6298's method, with
    150 ms as the floor, keeps direct peers exactly as today.

## 19. Measured on hardware before any of it is trusted

The gate. Every derived and chosen figure above stays derived or chosen
until the item that settles it has run. Boards: the WROOM as the board,
an ESP32-CAM (the static-DRAM floor), three or four plain ESP32s as
repeaters and leaves, a second board for item 12, and the bench's own
access point. Every item reads SYS's loop average, loop worst and
slow-pass count on the board before and after.

1. **f, the cost of one hop.** Timestamped one-frame round trips through
   0 to 3 repeaters (as many as the bench has), at 24 Mbps and forced
   1 Mbps, at 16 and 215 bytes, p50 and p95. Replaces §13's estimate.
2. **The board's callers' latency under mesh load.** Gateway ping and a
   telnet caller's echo while a picture crosses two repeaters and members
   PING. A regression against the same run without the mesh is a blocker;
   the share at which latency moves sets §15.6's "busy air".
3. **CALLIN echo end to end** at 1, 2 and 3 hops, against the chosen 50 ms;
   sets `mesh_call_depth`'s default.
4. **RSSI against loss, and bulk throughput.** Delivery against RSSI per
   hop (fits §7.3's penalties and §15.6's weak-link floor), and a picture's
   KB/s at each depth the bench reaches.
5. **Repair under a caller.** A repeater on a caller's path loses power
   mid-typing: pause, keys lost (must be 0), inside or outside 7 s, with
   and without an alternate parent.
6. **A router channel change** with the deepest mesh the bench can build:
   the time for each layer to be PLANNED, and the total. Moves
   `mesh_data_depth`'s default.
7. **An outsider**: rogue beacons at rank 1, spoofed origin ids, a proxy
   flood. Dropped and counted by reason; no path moves; loop unchanged.
8. **A captured member** dropping 100%, then 30%: routed around within one
   recompute, and the verdict "repeater losing frames" raised.
9. **The plan computation** on the WROOM's runner at 4, 16 and 32 members
   (synthetic reports), and whether it shows in the loop.
10. **Memory**: heap free and low with the mesh on and 32 pairings, WROOM
    and ESP32-CAM; static DRAM off the ELF of every env against the parent
    build (target 0, including the health roll-up).
11. **A soak**: four members, one carrying a door caller, 24 hours,
    repeaters power-cycled on a timer; health verdicts compared against
    what was done to the mesh.
12. **Two co-located meshes**: two boards on one router, each with its own
    repeaters, against one: the board's gateway latency and §4.2's
    arithmetic checked against the airtime the roll-up estimates.

**Depth beyond the bench's box count** is tested on the host only, with
the reach table (§17 item 12), up to 16 hops: that proves the format,
the plan and loop control at depth, and proves nothing about latency,
airtime or repair time, which stay derived until boxes exist to measure
them.

**Host tests, before any of the above**: forwarding at depths 2 to 16;
local failover and `detour`; PATH_ERR and the alternate; the rank check
against a stale plan; TTL at 0; every state timeout ending; a forged
origin id dropped as a tag failure; a beacon flood not evicting a parent;
proxied pairing refused unchecked; a session refused past
`mesh_call_depth` and kept when pushed past it; each health verdict
firing on the fault it names and on nothing else; MESH_STATS across a
counter wrap; the clock from 0x90000000. Every guard proved by removal.
